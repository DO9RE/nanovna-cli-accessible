Training Scenario 5: Low-Pass Filter (with S21 Transmission)

Training file: Training/05_tiefpass_filter.csv Frequency range: 144.000 - 146.000 MHz (2m amateur radio band) Difficulty level: ⭐⭐ Medium Learning objective: Understanding filters through S21 transmission measurement and S11+S21 combination

📋 Scenario Description

Fictional Component

You are measuring a low-pass filter for the 2m band. The filter is designed to pass frequencies up to ~145 MHz with minimal attenuation, but increasingly attenuates higher frequencies. This type of filter is used in transmitters for harmonic suppression.

Technical Characteristics

  • Impedance (S11): 50.1Ω + j0.1Ω (very well matched across entire band)
  • SWR: 1.00 to 1.09 (excellent!)
  • Return Loss (S11): 26.48 to 54.10 dB (very good)
  • Transmission (S21):
  • 144.0 MHz: -0.015 dB (99.5% passthrough)
  • 145.0 MHz: -0.10 dB (99.0% passthrough)
  • 146.0 MHz: -0.15 dB (98.5% passthrough)
  • Special feature: First training file with S21 data!

Physical Meaning

A low-pass filter typically consists of:

  • Series inductors: Block high frequencies
  • Parallel capacitors: Shunt high frequencies to ground
  • Design goal: Maximum passthrough in usable band, maximum attenuation above

Typical Applications:

  • Harmonic filters in HF transmitters (often legally required!)
  • EMC filters (protection against RF interference)
  • PA output filters (transceivers, power amplifiers)
  • Harmonic suppression for amateur radio transmitters

Importance:

  • Harmonics (2×f, 3×f, ...) can interfere with TV/radio reception
  • Legal limits for unwanted emissions
  • Good filters: < -40 dB attenuation at 2nd harmonic

🎵 Expected Audio Events

Reflection Parameters (S11) - Input Matching

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Extremely quiet, very low sustained tone (similar to Scenario 1!)
  • SWR increases very slightly from 1.00 to 1.09
  • Minimally audible upward trend
  • Nearly constant across entire band

Meaning:

  • Filter is perfectly matched to 50Ω system
  • Input impedance remains 50Ω (almost no reflection)
  • This is a quality characteristic of a good filter!
  • Difference from antennas: filter MUST be broadband matched

Position in stereo field:

  • Sound remains centered (same matching across band)
  • No significant left-right movement

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Very high tone (26-54 dB)
  • Slight variation, but consistently high
  • Tone "flutters" slightly over frequency

Meaning:

  • High return loss = Low reflection = Good matching
  • Filter reflects hardly any energy back to transmitter
  • Transmitter "sees" 50Ω load across entire band

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Constant medium tone around 50Ω
  • Increases minimally from 50.1Ω to 52.3Ω
  • Very stable triangle wave sound
  • Almost no perceptible pitch change

Meaning:

  • Impedance stays very close to 50Ω
  • Filter is resistive across entire band
  • No significant reactive component

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth)

What you should hear:

  • Extremely quiet tone around zero line
  • Increases minimally from +0.2Ω to +2.0Ω (barely audible)
  • Very slight upward trend (slightly inductive)
  • Remains in neutral range

Meaning:

  • Almost no reactance = Nearly purely resistive
  • Minimal inductance from filter coils
  • Excellent filter design!

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Very quiet tone near zero point
  • Minimal phase change across band
  • Phase remains nearly constant

Meaning:

  • Low phase = Resistance dominates
  • Filter causes minimal phase shift in passband

Transmission Parameters (S21) - Passthrough Characteristics ⭐

6. S21 Magnitude (Transmission Loss) - NEW!

Activate:

  • In acoustic mode: Key 6 or Key T

What you should hear:

  • Very quiet, slowly descending tone
  • Begins at almost inaudible level (-0.015 dB at 144 MHz)
  • Slightly descends over frequency
  • Ends at slightly lower, but still high tone (-0.15 dB at 146 MHz)

Meaning:

  • S21 = Transmission factor (how much signal passes through?)
  • High tone = Low attenuation = Much signal passes through
  • Descending tone = Low-pass characteristic!
  • Low frequencies: Better passed through
  • Higher frequencies: More attenuated
  • In 2m band: Only 0.15 dB attenuation = Excellent!

Comparison with other values:

S21 (dB)  | Transmission | What it means

----------|--------------|------------------ 0 dB      | 100%         | Perfect (impossible in practice) -0.1 dB   | 99%          | Excellent (this filter!) -1 dB     | 89%          | Very good -3 dB     | 50%          | Power halved -10 dB    | 10%          | Strong attenuation -40 dB    | 0.01%        | Almost complete blocking

Stereo position:

  • Sound moves slightly downward (frequency increases, attenuation increases)
  • Shows low-pass characteristic spatially

7. S21 Phase (Transmission Phase) - NEW!

Activate:

  • In acoustic mode: Key 7 or Key P

What you should hear:

  • Descending tone (phase becomes more negative)
  • Begins at approximately -1° to -2° (almost zero)
  • Descends to approximately -15° to -20° at 146 MHz
  • Continuous downward trend

Meaning:

  • S21 Phase = Phase delay through filter
  • Negative phase = Signal delayed (normal property of filters)
  • Increasing delay = Group delay of filter
  • Linear phase over frequency = Good filter design

Practical meaning:

  • Low phase delay = Minimal signal distortion
  • Important for digital transmission (RTTY, FT8, etc.)
  • SSB telephony: Usually not critical

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

What You Learn from This Scenario

1. Understanding S-Parameter Concept

  • S11: Reflection at input (as with antennas)
  • S21: Transmission from Port 1 to Port 2 (only for two-ports!)
  • S12: Reverse transmission (not measured here)
  • S22: Reflection at output (not measured here)

Key principle:

  • One-ports (antennas): Only S11
  • Two-ports (filters, amplifiers, cables): S11 + S21 (+ S12 + S22)

2. Filter Quality Criteria

Good low-pass:

  1. Low insertion loss in passband (< 1 dB)
  2. Good matching (SWR < 1.5) across passband
  3. Steep rolloff (attenuation increases rapidly above cutoff frequency)
  4. High rejection in stopband (> 40 dB at 2nd harmonic)

3. Acoustic Signatures for Filters

  • S11 (SWR): Should be low and constant (good matching)
  • S21 Magnitude:
  • Low-pass: Descending with frequency
  • High-pass: Ascending with frequency
  • Band-pass: Peak in middle, flanks left/right
  • Band-stop: Dip in middle
  • S21 Phase: Usually linearly descending (group delay)

4. Difference: Matching vs. Passthrough

  • Good matching (S11) does NOT automatically mean good passthrough (S21)!
  • Example: Perfectly matched attenuator (50Ω)
  • S11: Perfect (SWR=1.0)
  • S21: Poor (-10 dB or more)
  • Both parameters are important!

5. Practical Application

  • Transmitter without low-pass: Harmonics interfere with neighbors (legally problematic!)
  • Transmitter with low-pass: Clean, legal, neighbor-friendly
  • Measurement: S11 + S21 completely characterize filter
  • Design: Trade-off between attenuation, rolloff, size, cost

⚠️ Important Notes

Reality Check: Filters in Practice

Typical Insertion Loss:

  • Professional filters: 0.1 - 0.5 dB
  • Amateur homebrew: 0.5 - 1.5 dB
  • Cheap filters: 1 - 3 dB
  • Acceptable for QRP: < 1 dB
  • Acceptable for QRO: < 0.5 dB

Example calculation:

Transmitter: 100W

Filter: 0.15 dB attenuation Loss: 100W × (1 - 10^(-0.15/10)) = 100W × 0.034 = 3.4W  Result: 96.6W reaches antenna → Acceptable!

Harmonic attenuation:

  • Legal requirement (EU): < -40 dBc (dB relative to carrier)
  • Good practice: < -50 dBc
  • Excellent: < -60 dBc

Measurement Technique Aspect

S21 measurement requires:

  1. Second port on VNA (NanoVNA has 2 ports!)
  2. Through calibration (connect Port 1 directly to Port 2)
  3. Isolation calibration (leave ports open)
  4. Filter between ports connected

Calibration sequence:

  1. OPEN (both ports open)
  2. SHORT (both ports shorted)
  3. LOAD (both ports terminated with 50Ω)
  4. THROUGH (Port 1 directly to Port 2)
  5. ISOLATION (ports open, measurement of crosstalk)

Filter Design Basics

Low-pass topologies:

  1. LC ladder filter: Inexpensive, simple, low-loss
  2. Pi-filter: Compact, good impedance matching
  3. T-filter: Similar to Pi-filter
  4. Chebyshev: Steep rolloff, ripple in passband
  5. Butterworth: Flat passband, less steep rolloff
  6. Bessel: Linear phase (important for digital modes)

Cutoff frequency vs. usable frequency:

  • Cutoff frequency (-3 dB): Where power is halved
  • Usable range: Should be well BEFORE cutoff frequency
  • Rule of thumb: Cutoff frequency ≥ 1.5 × max. usable frequency

🔬 Deep Dive: S-Parameter Theory

What are S-Parameters?

Definition:

S-Parameters = Scattering Parameters

S21 = b2 / a1  (when a2 = 0) b2 = Wave coming out of Port 2 a1 = Wave going into Port 1  → S21 describes transmission from Port 1 to Port 2

In Decibels:

S21 (dB) = 20 × log10(|S21|)

Examples: |S21| = 1.0   → S21 = 0 dB    (perfect, no attenuation) |S21| = 0.99  → S21 ≈ -0.1 dB (this filter!) |S21| = 0.5   → S21 = -6 dB   (half voltage) |S21| = 0.1   → S21 = -20 dB  (strongly attenuated)

In Power:

P_out / P_in = |S21|²

Example: S21 = -0.15 dB |S21| = 10^(-0.15/20) = 0.983 P_out / P_in = 0.983² = 0.966 = 96.6%  → 3.4% power is absorbed in filter

Why is S21 Important?

  1. Filters: S21 shows frequency response (passthrough/stopband)
  2. Amplifiers: S21 shows gain (e.g., +20 dB)
  3. Cables: S21 shows attenuation (frequency-dependent)
  4. Switches: S21 shows isolation (ON vs. OFF)
  5. Mixers: S21 shows conversion loss

Practical tip:

  • If you want to know "attenuation" or "gain" → Measure S21!
  • If you want to know "matching" → Measure S11!
