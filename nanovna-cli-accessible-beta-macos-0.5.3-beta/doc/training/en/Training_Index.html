NanoVNA CLI Accessible - Training Suite

Version: 1.0 Date: February 2026 Language: English Target Audience: Blind and visually impaired amateur radio operators

📚 Overview

This comprehensive training suite was developed to teach blind and visually impaired amateur radio operators how to work with the NanoVNA CLI Accessible tool. It contains 10 realistic measurement scenarios with complete CSV files and detailed training documents.

🎯 Learning Objectives

After completing this training suite, you will be able to:

  • ✅ Acoustically distinguish between perfect and mismatched antennas
  • ✅ Immediately recognize inductive and capacitive behavior by sound
  • ✅ Spatially hear and navigate the Smith chart
  • ✅ Analyze filters and cables using S21 parameters
  • ✅ Perform TDR fault location (short circuit/open line)
  • ✅ Characterize multi-band antennas
  • ✅ Confidently apply all U-Menu comfort functions
  • ✅ Create and interpret professional measurement reports

📋 Training Scenarios - Overview

🟢 Beginner Level (Scenarios 1-3)

Scenario 1: Perfect 50Ω Resonant Antenna ⭐

File: Training/01_perfekte_resonanz_50ohm.csv Documentation: doc/Training_01_Perfekte_Resonanz.md Duration: 15-20 minutes What you'll learn:

  • What does a perfectly tuned antenna sound like?
  • Internalize reference audio for SWR = 1.0
  • Acoustically experience Smith chart center
  • Baseline for all further comparisons

Key findings:

  • SWR curve: Constant, very low tone
  • Reactance: Almost inaudible, floating around zero
  • Smith position: Exactly central, no movement
  • U-Menu: "No matching required"

Scenario 2: Antenna Too Short (Inductive) ⭐⭐

File: Training/02_antenne_zu_kurz_induktiv.csv Documentation: doc/Training_02_Antenne_Zu_Kurz.md Duration: 20-25 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Acoustically recognize inductive mismatch
  • Positive reactance = Front in Smith chart
  • SWR falls to the right = Resonance above band
  • Diagnosis: "Lengthen antenna"

Key findings:

  • Reactance curve: Falling, but in positive range
  • Smith position: FRONT (inductive), moves backward
  • Polar rotation: Counterclockwise, approaching center
  • U-Menu: Suggests series capacitor

Scenario 3: Antenna Too Long (Capacitive) ⭐⭐

File: Training/03_antenne_zu_lang_kapazitiv.csv Documentation: doc/Training_03_Antenne_Zu_Lang.md Duration: 20-25 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Capacitive mismatch = opposite of inductive
  • Negative reactance = Back in Smith chart
  • SWR rises to the right = Resonance below band
  • Diagnosis: "Shorten antenna"

Key findings:

  • Reactance curve: Falling, but in negative range
  • Smith position: BACK (capacitive), moves further back
  • Polar rotation: Clockwise, moving away from center
  • U-Menu: Suggests series inductor

🟡 Advanced Level (Scenarios 4-7)

Scenario 4: Lambda/4 Cable Transformer ⭐⭐⭐

File: Training/04_lambda_viertel_kabel.csv Documentation: doc/Training_04_Lambda_Viertel_Kabel.md Duration: 30-35 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Impedance transformation by λ/4-line
  • Spectacular circular rotation in Smith chart
  • Transforms from 25Ω to 100Ω at λ/4
  • Frequency dependence of transformation

Key findings:

  • Smith polar mode: Full 180° rotation audible!
  • SWR: U-shaped course (high-low-high)
  • Phase: Passes through all quadrants
  • Practical use: Antenna matching, stubs

Scenario 5: Low-Pass Filter ⭐⭐⭐

File: Training/05_tiefpass_filter.csv Documentation: doc/Training_05_Tiefpass_Filter.md Duration: 25-30 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Use S21 parameters for the first time
  • Acoustically distinguish passband vs. stopband
  • Filter characteristics: cutoff frequency, roll-off
  • Combine S11 + S21 for complete filter analysis

Key findings:

  • S21: Dramatically drops above cutoff frequency
  • S11: Rises = reflection in stopband
  • U-Menu filter analysis: automatically find -3 dB cutoff frequency
  • Practical: Check harmonic suppression

Scenario 6: Multiband Antenna (6m/4m/2m) ⭐⭐⭐

File: Training/06_multiband_antenne.csv Documentation: doc/Training_06_Multiband_Antenne.md Duration: 35-40 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Acoustically identify multiple resonances
  • Harmonic relationships between bands
  • Band suitability over wide frequency ranges
  • Complex Smith movement patterns

Key findings:

  • SWR: Three distinct "valleys" at resonances
  • U-Menu resonance search: finds all three minima
  • Smith: Multiple passes through center
  • Band suitability: 50 MHz, 70 MHz, 145 MHz, 225 MHz, 435 MHz

Scenario 7: Cable with Attenuation (S21 Analysis) ⭐⭐⭐

File: Training/07_kabel_mit_daempfung.csv Documentation: doc/Training_07_Kabel_Daempfung.md Duration: 25-30 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Measure and calculate cable attenuation
  • Frequency dependence of attenuation
  • S11 vs. S21: What tells what?
  • Judge cable quality

Key findings:

  • S11: Perfect matching (SWR = 1.0)
  • S21: Linearly falling with frequency (typical for cables)
  • U-Menu: Calculate attenuation per meter
  • Comparison with manufacturer specifications possible

🔴 Expert Level (Scenarios 8-10)

Scenario 8: Short Circuit at 10m (TDR) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

File: Training/08_kurzschluss_10m.csv Documentation: doc/Training_08_Kurzschluss.md Duration: 30-35 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) principle
  • Recognize short circuit signature
  • Calculate fault distance (10 meters)
  • Practical fault finding in installations

Key findings:

  • SWR: Extremely high (> 10:1), periodic fluctuation
  • Reactance: Strongly negative (capacitive with short circuit!)
  • Smith: At the edge, circling
  • U-Menu TDR: "Short circuit at approx. 10m"

Scenario 9: Open Line at 5m (TDR) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

File: Training/09_offene_leitung_5m.csv Documentation: doc/Training_09_Offene_Leitung.md Duration: 30-35 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Open line = opposite of short circuit
  • Very high impedance (> 1000Ω)
  • TDR distinction: Open vs. short
  • Practical: Find connector problems

Key findings:

  • SWR: Extremely high, but different periodicity than short circuit
  • Reactance: Strongly positive (inductive with open line!)
  • Impedance |Z|: Very high (thousands of ohms)
  • U-Menu TDR: "Open line at approx. 5m"

Scenario 10: Bandpass Filter for 2m ⭐⭐⭐⭐

File: Training/10_bandpass_filter_2m.csv Documentation: doc/Training_10_Bandpass_Filter.md Duration: 35-40 minutes What you'll learn:

  • Bandpass = combination of lowpass + highpass
  • Passband with low attenuation
  • Stopbands below and above
  • Professional filter characterization

Key findings:

  • S21: Peak shape in passband (145-146 MHz)
  • S11: Low in passband, high in stopbands
  • U-Menu: Upper and lower cutoff frequency
  • Bandwidth, insertion loss, Q factor automatic

🛠️ Using the Training Suite

Import Training Files

  1. Navigate to main menu
  2. Press L key (Load)
  3. Browse file list with arrow keys
  4. Select desired training CSV
  5. Press Enter to load
  6. Confirmation: "XX measurement points successfully loaded"

Start Acoustic Mode

  1. Press A key (Acoustic Analysis)
  2. Configuration if needed: Press A (Audio Configuration)
  • Select engine (Synthesizer / MIDI)
  • Calibrate surround (Press W - Assistant)
  1. Press SPACE to play
  2. Smith visualization: Activate V key, B for mode

Use All Functions

Curve Control

  • Keys 1-5: Enable/disable curves
  • Ctrl + 1-5: Decrease volume
  • Shift + 1-5: Increase volume
  • T key: Toggle smooth/dotted mode

Navigation

  • Arrow keys ←/→: Change position
  • Arrow keys ↑/↓: Adjust jump width
  • G key: Go to (frequency/position)

Loop Function

  • L key: Set left marker
  • R key: Set right marker
  • O key: Loop on/off
  • Z key: Loop zoom
  • I key: Invert loop

Helper Tools

  • Y key: Y-axis ruler (value range)
  • X key: X-axis blips (position)
  • N key: Status line on/off
  • H key: Show help

U-Menu Analyses

  • ESC key → Back to main menu
  • U key → U-Menu comfort functions
  • Select and execute functions 1-12

🔊 Audio Settings Recommendations

Synthesizer Engine (Default)

Recommended for:

  • Precise pitch discrimination
  • Technical analysis
  • Beginners

Advantages:

  • Clear, distinct tones
  • Easily distinguishable waveforms
  • Low CPU demand

MIDI Engine

Recommended for:

  • Musical perception
  • Long analysis sessions
  • Advanced users

Advantages:

  • More pleasant for long sessions
  • Rich timbres
  • Memorable instrument assignments

Smith Audio Surround Configuration

For Stereo Users (2.0)

Optimal settings (after calibration assistant):

  • Crossfeed: 30-50%
  • Rear attenuation: 15-25%
  • Side emphasis: 120-140%

Tip: Use calibration assistant! (A → C → W)

For 5.1 Surround Users

Optimal settings:

  • Front/rear distance: 100-120%
  • Side emphasis: 110-130%
  • V/H separation: 150-180%
  • Center strength: 40-60%

For 7.1 Surround Users

Optimal settings:

  • All distances: 100-110%
  • V/H separation: 130-150%
  • Side emphasis: 100-120% (7.1 has real side speakers!)
  • Center strength: 30-50%

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

General

Q: How long does the complete training suite take? A: Approx. 8-10 hours of pure learning time spread over 3-4 weeks. Depends on prior knowledge and learning pace. Q: Must I work through all scenarios in order? A: Scenarios 1-3 should be sequential (basics). After that you can choose by interest. Q: Can I practice with own measurements before I have a real NanoVNA? A: Yes! These training files are exactly for that. Learn the tool without hardware.

Technical

Q: Why don't I hear Smith audio cues? A: Check:

  1. Smith visualization active? (V key)
  2. Surround volume in audio config (A → C → S)
  3. At least stereo playback active?

Q: Smith position sounds different than described? A: Run through calibration assistant (A → C → W). It optimizes for your hearing! Q: Curves are too quiet/loud? A: Ctrl + 1-5 (quieter) or Shift + 1-5 (louder). Individual volume per curve.

Learning

Q: I don't understand Smith chart position? A:

  1. Start with Mode 3 (Impedance Direct) - most intuitive
  2. Then Mode 1 (Cartesian)
  3. Mode 2 (Polar) for advanced users

Q: How do I remember inductive vs. capacitive? A: Memory aids:

  • Front = Forward = Inductive (coil) = Too short
  • Back = Behind = Capacitive (capacitor) = Too long
  • Positive = Plus = Inductive
  • Negative = Negative = Capacitive

Q: What's the most important curve? A: Reactance (Key 4) is usually most informative!

  • Positive/negative → Inductive/capacitive
  • Rising/falling → Direction of detuning
