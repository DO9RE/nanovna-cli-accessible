Training Scenario 3: Antenna Too Long (Capacitive)

Training file: Training/03_antenne_zu_lang_kapazitiv.csv Frequency range: 144.000 - 146.000 MHz (2m amateur radio band) Difficulty level: ⭐⭐ Medium (Advanced beginners) Learning objective: Understand how capacitive mismatch sounds - opposite of Scenario 2

📋 Scenario Description

Fictitious Component

You are measuring a 2m Yagi antenna that is too long. The radiator is approximately 3-5 cm longer than optimal. This leads to capacitive behavior - the antenna acts like a capacitor with a resistor connected in series.

Technical Characteristics (at 144 MHz)

  • Impedance: 52.8Ω - j0.5Ω (almost perfect!)
  • SWR: 1.01 (excellent)
  • At 146 MHz: 35.2Ω - j28.5Ω (capacitive!)
  • Reactance (X): -0.5Ω at 144 MHz → decreasing → -28.5Ω at 146 MHz
  • Resistance (R): 52.8Ω at 144 MHz → decreasing → 35.2Ω at 146 MHz
  • Physical behavior: Antenna becomes "electrically shorter" with increasing frequency

Physical Meaning

Negative Reactance (X < 0) = Capacitive:

  • Antenna too long = Too much "electrical length"
  • Voltage lags behind current (as with a capacitor)
  • With increasing frequency, antenna becomes electrically even longer → reactance becomes even more negative
  • Resonance was already at approximately 144 MHz (X ≈ 0)

Typical Causes:

  • Radiator mechanically cut too long
  • Frequency shifted downward due to capacitive loading
  • Environmental influences (buildings, trees near antenna)
  • Moisture in insulation

⚠️ IMPORTANT - Mirror Image of Scenario 2: This measurement is exactly the opposite of Scenario 2! The values at 144 MHz here correspond to the values at 146 MHz there, and vice versa.

🎵 Expected Audio Events

1. SWR Curve (Key 1) - Opposite of Scenario 2!

What you should hear:

  • Low tone at 144 MHz (SWR 1.01 - excellent!)
  • Tone becomes continuously higher across frequency
  • Clear upward trend from left to right
  • At 146 MHz: High tone (SWR 1.95)

Stereo Position:

  • Left (144 MHz): Very quiet, low pitch (good!)
  • Right (146 MHz): Clearly audible, higher pitch (worse)
  • Movement: Clearly upward (getting worse)

Meaning:

  • SWR rises with increasing frequency ⚠️ REVERSE of Scenario 2!
  • Resonance frequency is below 144 MHz
  • Antenna becomes worse matched at higher frequencies
  • Diagnosis: Antenna too long → must be shortened

Comparison with Scenario 2:

  • Scenario 2: SWR falls (gets better) → Too short
  • Scenario 3: SWR rises (gets worse) → Too long
  • Memory aid: Rising = too long (gets even longer)

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2)

What you should hear:

  • Very high tone at 144 MHz (120 dB = excellent)
  • Tone decreases continuously across frequency
  • Strong downward trend from left to right
  • At 146 MHz: Low tone (8.54 dB = moderate)

Meaning:

  • Return loss decreases = More reflection at higher frequencies
  • Correlates with SWR deterioration
  • Mirror image of Scenario 2

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3)

What you should hear:

  • Medium tone at 144 MHz (53Ω)
  • Tone decreases slightly across frequency
  • At 146 MHz: Lower tone (35Ω)
  • Downward movement

Meaning:

  • Impedance magnitude decreases from 53Ω → 35Ω
  • Crosses 50Ω mark somewhere in the band
  • Opposite of Scenario 2 (where it rose)

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth) ⚡ KEY CURVE!

What you should hear:

  • Clearly audible, falling sawtooth tone
  • Strong downward trend from left to right
  • Left (144 MHz): Almost zero (-0.5Ω = nearly resistive)
  • Right (146 MHz): Deep tone (-28.5Ω = strongly capacitive)
  • Clear, continuous downward movement into negative

Sawtooth Character:

  • With negative reactance: Sawtooth falls downward (falling edge)
  • The more negative, the deeper the tone
  • Opposite to Scenario 2 (where sawtooth rose from high to low)

Meaning: ⭐ DIAGNOSTIC SIGNAL!

  • Negative reactance = Capacitive = Antenna too long
  • Falling reactance into negative = Antenna becomes "longer" at higher frequency
  • Zero crossing below the band = Resonance at < 144 MHz
  • Diagnosis clear: Shorten radiator!

Comparison with Scenario 2:

  • Scenario 2: Reactance positive, falls from +28.5Ω → +0.5Ω (inductive)
  • Scenario 3: Reactance negative, falls from -0.5Ω → -28.5Ω (capacitive)
  • Memory aid: Negative = Capacitive = Capacitor = too long

5. Phase (Key 5)

What you should hear:

  • Slightly negative phase at 144 MHz (approx. -2°)
  • Phase decreases across frequency (becomes more negative)
  • Reaches approx. -38° at 146 MHz

Meaning:

  • Negative phase = Voltage lags (capacitive)
  • Decreasing phase = Enhancement of capacitive behavior
  • Opposite of Scenario 2 (there positive, decreasing phase)

🎯 Smith Chart Audio Visualization

Mode 1: Cartesian - OPPOSITE of Scenario 2!

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts ALMOST IN CENTER, slightly BACK
  • Continuous movement to FAR BACK-LEFT
  • Sound moves away from center as frequency increases
  • At the end: Far back-left from you

Position Meaning:

  • Back = Im(Γ) < 0 = Negative reactance = Capacitive
  • Left = Re(Γ) < 0 = Resistance below 50Ω
  • Movement further back = Reactance becomes more negative
  • Movement to the left = Resistance decreases

Acoustic Landmarks:

  1. Start (144 MHz): Almost central, very slightly back → "Almost perfect"
  2. Middle (145 MHz): Clearly back-left → "Becoming capacitive"
  3. End (146 MHz): Far back-left → "Strongly capacitive, low impedance"

Comparison with Scenario 2:

  • Scenario 2: Starts front-left → moves to central (inductive → better)
  • Scenario 3: Starts central-back → moves to back-left (capacitive → worse)
  • Mirror image! Exactly opposite!

Axis Crossings:

  • Horizontal axis (R = 50Ω): Crossed at approximately 144.8 MHz
  • Vertical axis (X = 0Ω): Crossed at approximately 144.0 MHz (at the beginning!)
  • Meaning: Resonance directly at band start!

Mode 2: Polar

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts almost centered near you
  • Sound "wanders" clockwise to the right
  • Simultaneously: Sound becomes louder (further away from you)
  • Distance increases = SWR deteriorates

Rotation Meaning:

  • Angle ∠Γ: Starts at approximately -177° (almost back)
  • Angle decreases (toward -147°, so to back-right)
  • Shows phase change of reflection coefficient
  • Clockwise = Typical with capacitive detuning

Radius Meaning:

  • |Γ|: Starts at approximately 0.01 (very close to center)
  • Radius increases to approximately 0.32 (moderately far from center)
  • Audible: Sound becomes louder, moves away from you

Comparison with Scenario 2:

  • Scenario 2: Counterclockwise, approach (inductive, getting better)
  • Scenario 3: Clockwise, distance (capacitive, getting worse)

Mode 3: Impedance Direct

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts CENTRAL-RIGHT and SLIGHTLY BACK
  • Movement to LEFT (R decreases from 53Ω → 35Ω)
  • Movement to FAR BACK (X decreases from -0.5Ω → -28.5Ω)
  • Center (50Ω, 0Ω) is left behind

Position Mapping:

  • Start: Slightly right (R = 53Ω) and slightly back (X = -0.5Ω)
  • End: Left (R = 35Ω) and far back (X = -28.5Ω)
  • Movement away from center = Getting worse

Diagnosis:

  • Start almost central = Very good match at band start
  • Movement to back-left = R and X deteriorate
  • Interpretation: Resonance below band, antenna too long

Mode 4: SWR Circles

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts VERY CLOSE to you
  • Continuous distancing from you
  • Volume increases with deteriorating match
  • At the end: Sound FAR AWAY from you (high SWR)

Meaning:

  • Distance = SWR level
  • Close to you = SWR 1.01 (at 144 MHz) = Perfect!
  • Far away = SWR 1.95 (at 146 MHz) = Moderate
  • Opposite of Scenario 2 (there approach)

🔧 Exercises and Learning Tasks

Exercise 1: Compare Scenarios 2 and 3 Directly

Goal: Learn to distinguish inductive vs. capacitive

  1. Load Scenario 2 and hear reactance curve (Key 4)
  • Note: Falls from high (+28.5Ω) to low (+0.5Ω)
  • Position: Positive range, sawtooth rising
  1. Load Scenario 3 and hear reactance curve
  • Note: Falls from almost zero (-0.5Ω) to deeply negative (-28.5Ω)
  • Position: Negative range, sawtooth descending
  1. Clearly hear the difference:
  • Scenario 2: In positive range (inductive)
  • Scenario 3: In negative range (capacitive)

Exercise 2: Compare Smith Movement Direction

Goal: Understand front vs. back position

  1. Scenario 2: Smith Mode 1
  • Sound starts FRONT (inductive)
  • Moves to BACK (getting better)
  1. Scenario 3: Smith Mode 1
  • Sound starts BACK (capacitive)
  • Moves even FURTHER BACK (getting worse)
  1. Remember:
  • FRONT = Inductive = Positive = Antenna too short
  • BACK = Capacitive = Negative = Antenna too long

Exercise 3: Recognize SWR Slope

Goal: Slope = Diagnosis

  1. Scenario 2: SWR curve (Key 1)
  • Tone falls → Gets better → Resonance above band → Too short
  1. Scenario 3: SWR curve
  • Tone rises → Gets worse → Resonance below band → Too long
  1. Diagnostic Rule:
  • SWR falls to the right → Lengthen antenna
  • SWR rises to the right → Shorten antenna

Exercise 4: Polar Rotation Direction

Goal: Clockwise vs. counterclockwise

  1. Scenario 2: Smith Mode 2 (Polar)
  • Rotation counterclockwise
  • Sound approaches you
  • Remember: Inductive → Counter-CW → Getting better
  1. Scenario 3: Smith Mode 2
  • Rotation clockwise
  • Sound moves away from you
  • Remember: Capacitive → CW → Getting worse

🎓 Learning Objectives - Recognize Capacitive

Acoustic Signatures of "Too Long / Capacitive"

1. Reactance Audio Pattern

  • Falling, but in NEGATIVE range
  • Starts near zero, ends deeply negative
  • Sawtooth tone "sinks below zero line"
  • Diagnosis: Negative + Falling = Too long!

2. SWR Audio Pattern

  • Low tone, rising = SWR low, gets worse
  • Minimum at lower band edge
  • Diagnosis: SWR rises to right = Shorten!

3. Smith Audio Pattern (Cartesian)

  • Starts back or central
  • Moves BACK
  • Moves away from center
  • Diagnosis: Back position = Capacitive!

4. Smith Audio Pattern (Polar)

  • Rotation CLOCKWISE
  • Distance from center increases
  • Diagnosis: CW + Distance = Capacitive, getting worse

Physical Understanding

Why is an antenna that is too long capacitive?

  1. Electrical length > λ/4: Antenna electrically too long
  2. Current maximum beyond feed point
  3. Reactance negative: Capacitive reactance dominates
  4. Analogy to capacitor: Oversized resonance structure acts capacitively

Why does it get worse at higher frequency?

  1. λ becomes smaller: Wavelength decreases
  2. Antenna becomes "electrically even longer": Fixed length / smaller λ = larger fraction
  3. Moves away from λ/4: Becomes even more "too long"
  4. Resonance was below: At approximately 143-143.5 MHz
