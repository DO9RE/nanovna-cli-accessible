Training Scenario 1: Perfect 50Ω Resonance Antenna

Training file: Training/01_perfekte_resonanz_50ohm.csv Frequency range: 144.000 - 146.000 MHz (2m amateur radio band) Difficulty level: ⭐ Easy (Beginner) Learning objective: Understand how a perfectly tuned antenna sounds

📋 Scenario Description

Fictitious Component

You are measuring an ideally tuned 2m Yagi antenna for the 2-meter amateur radio band (144-146 MHz). The antenna has been precisely tuned to the band center at approx. 145 MHz. The radiator has the exact length so that the impedance remains close to 50Ω across the entire band.

Technical Properties

  • Impedance: 50Ω ±0.1Ω (almost perfectly resistive)
  • SWR: 1.00 to 1.003 (excellent!)
  • Return Loss: > 46 dB (outstanding)
  • Reactance (X): ±0.2Ω (practically zero - purely resistive)
  • Resistance (R): 49.9 to 50.1Ω (very stable)
  • Reflection coefficient: |Γ| < 0.003 (negligible)

Physical Meaning

This is the ideal scenario in antenna technology! Such a match is achieved through:

  • Exact radiator length
  • Correct feedpoint position
  • High-quality materials
  • Professional tuning
  • Optimal mechanical construction

In practice, such a perfect match is rare - this training shows you the theoretical optimum.

🎧 Acoustic Analysis

Import and load file

  1. Main menu: Press key L (Load)
  2. List of files appears - navigate with arrow keys
  3. Select 01_perfekte_resonanz_50ohm.csv
  4. Press Enter to load
  5. Confirmation: "41 measurement points successfully loaded"

Start acoustic mode

  1. Main menu: Press key A (Acoustic Analysis)
  2. You are now in acoustic analysis mode
  3. Press SPACEBAR to start playback

🎵 Expected Audio Events

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Extremely quiet, very deep continuous tone
  • The tone barely changes across frequency
  • Pitch remains practically constant at the lowest level
  • No fluctuations perceivable

Meaning:

  • Lowest pitch = SWR near 1.0 (perfect!)
  • Constant tone = Consistent match across entire band
  • This is the reference audio for "ideal antenna"

Position in stereo field:

  • Sound remains centered (same match across whole band)
  • No left-right movement

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Very high, constant tone (> 46 dB)
  • Pitch remains extremely high and stable
  • Minimally audible "flutter" around 145 MHz (slight variation 46-60 dB)

Meaning:

  • High pitch = High return loss = Little reflection
  • Constancy = Consistently good match
  • Slight flutter at 145 MHz = Optimal point (up to 60 dB or more)

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Constant medium tone around 50Ω
  • Almost no pitch change (49.9 - 50.1Ω)
  • Characteristic triangle sound, but very stable

Meaning:

  • Medium pitch = Impedance around system impedance (50Ω)
  • Stability = Impedance remains constant across frequency
  • Perfect for transmitter matching

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth)

What you should hear:

  • Extremely quiet, almost inaudible tone around zero line
  • Tone "floats" around the middle (±0.2Ω)
  • No clear upward or downward movement
  • Sound remains in neutral range

Meaning:

  • Zero line = Purely resistive impedance (no inductive/capacitive component)
  • No movement = Antenna is neither too short (inductive) nor too long (capacitive)
  • This is the acoustic "sweet spot" signal

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Very quiet tone near zero point (±2°)
  • Minimal pitch fluctuations
  • Phase remains practically constant

Meaning:

  • Zero phase = Resistance dominates, reactance negligible
  • No large phase jumps = Stable, broadband match

🎯 Smith Chart Audio Visualization

Activate Smith visualization

  1. In acoustic mode: Press key V (Smith visualization on/off)
  2. Press key B to select mode:
  • Recommended for beginners: Mode 1 (Cartesian)
  • Alternative: Mode 3 (Impedance Direct)

Expected Smith Audio Cues

Mode 1: Cartesian (Recommended)

What you should hear:

  • Sound stays EXACTLY IN THE CENTER of the space
  • Position: Directly in front of you, at medium height
  • No movement left/right (Re(Γ) ≈ 0)
  • No movement forward/backward (Im(Γ) ≈ 0)
  • Sound "sticks" to the center = You are AT the optimal point

Meaning:

  • Center position = Perfect 50Ω match (You are AT the target!)
  • No movement = Reflection coefficient near zero
  • This is the "home" feeling in the Smith chart

Audio test:

  • Press V several times (on/off) and compare
  • WITHOUT Smith: Curves play normally
  • WITH Smith: Subtle noise/ambient exactly centrally positioned

Mode 3: Impedance Direct

What you should hear:

  • Sound exactly in the MIDDLE (R = 50Ω → central)
  • No forward/backward movement (X = 0Ω → central)
  • Position remains stable in neutral space

Meaning:

  • Middle position horizontally = 50Ω resistance
  • Middle position vertically = 0Ω reactance
  • Perfect centering = Perfect impedance match

Center Pulse (Reference Signal)

Activate:

  1. Key A in acoustic mode → Audio configuration
  2. Menu: C → Smith Audio → Activate Center Pulse

What you hear:

  • Periodic pulse from the CENTER
  • Your measurement should stay exactly there!
  • If you hear the center pulse and your measurement simultaneously → PERFECT!

🔧 Exercises and Learning Tasks

Exercise 1: Listen to curves individually

Goal: Get to know each curve in isolation

  1. Turn off all curves: Press keys 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 one after another
  2. Turn on only SWR: Key 1
  3. Press SPACEBAR and listen carefully
  4. Question: How deep is the tone? → Very deep = SWR near 1.0
  5. Repeat for curves 2-5 individually

Exercise 2: Comparison with other scenarios

Goal: Internalize reference audio

  1. Listen completely through this scenario
  2. Then load scenario 2 (too short antenna) and compare
  3. Notice differences:
  • Perfect antenna: All curves calm, central
  • Mismatched antenna: Curves move

Exercise 3: Compare Smith modes

Goal: Understand different Smith representations

  1. Activate Smith Mode 1 (Cartesian)
  2. Note position: Center
  3. Activate Smith Mode 2 (Polar)
  4. Note position: Center, no rotation
  5. Activate Smith Mode 4 (SWR Circles)
  6. Note position: Close to you (low SWR)

Exercise 4: Use Y-axis ruler

Goal: Understand pitch-to-value mapping

  1. Press Key Y (Y-axis ruler)
  2. You hear ascending scale from min to max value
  3. For SWR curve (Key 1): Deepest tone = 1.0, highest tone = Maximum in data
  4. Compare: Your measurement = deepest tone of the scale!

Exercise 5: Set loop markers

Goal: Analyze specific frequency range

  1. Use arrow keys to navigate
  2. At 145.0 MHz (band center): Key L (Left marker)
  3. At 145.5 MHz: Key R (Right marker)
  4. Key O (Activate loop)
  5. Playback focuses on band center → Even more stable!

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

What you learn from this scenario

1. Reference audio for perfect match

  • SWR: Deepest possible tone, constant
  • Return Loss: Highest tone, stable
  • Reactance: Inaudible, floating around zero
  • Smith position: Exactly central, no movement

2. Understanding of Smith chart center

  • Cartesian mode: Center = You stand at the target
  • Polar mode: No circular movement = Perfect match
  • SWR circle mode: Close to you = Low SWR

3. Acoustic landmarks

  • When all curves are "calm" and "centered" → Good match
  • When reactance inaudible → Purely resistive
  • When Smith central → 50Ω achieved

4. Practical reference

  • Such values are target values when tuning antennas
  • In reality: Usually SWR 1.1 - 1.3 → Still very good
  • This training = Ideal reference for comparison

⚠️ Important Notes

Reality check

In practice:

  • SWR 1.0 across 2 MHz band is extremely rare
  • SWR < 1.5 across band is already outstanding
  • SWR < 2.0 across band is well usable

This training shows:

  • Theoretical optimum
  • Reference for comparison with real measurements
  • Goal when tuning (often not achievable, but orientation)

Technical details

Return Loss > 46 dB means:

  • Less than 0.5% of power is reflected
  • 99.5% of power reaches the antenna
  • Practically no losses from mismatch

|Γ| < 0.003 means:

  • Reflection coefficient near zero
  • In Smith chart: Point lies exactly in center
  • VSWR (Voltage Standing Wave Ratio) = 1.006
