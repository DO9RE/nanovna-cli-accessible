Training Scenario 10: Bandpass Filter for 2m Band

Training file: Training/10_bandpass_filter_2m.csv Frequency range: 140.000 - 153.000 MHz (2m band + surroundings) Difficulty level: ⭐⭐⭐ Advanced Learning objective: Understand bandpass characteristics, identify filter passband and stopband acoustically

📋 Scenario Description

Fictional Component

You are measuring a bandpass filter for the 2m band (144-148 MHz). This filter is designed to pass only the 2m band with minimal attenuation while strongly attenuating frequencies outside the band. Such filters are used in multi-band transceivers, receivers (preselector), and repeater combiners.

Technical Characteristics

  • Design frequency: 145.5 MHz (band center)
  • Passband: ~144-147 MHz (-3 dB bandwidth ~3 MHz)
  • Impedance (S11): Well matched in passband, poorly matched outside
  • In-band: SWR ~1.01-1.05 (excellent!)
  • Out-of-band: SWR up to 9.0 (high reflection = desired for filter!)
  • Transmission (S21):
  • 140 MHz: -20 dB (1% transmission = strong rejection!)
  • 145.5 MHz: -0.01 dB (99.8% transmission = minimal attenuation)
  • 152 MHz: -40 dB (0.01% = almost complete rejection)
  • Special feature: Peak in the center with steep flanks on left/right!

Physical Meaning

A bandpass filter combines:

  • High-pass: Blocks low frequencies
  • Low-pass: Blocks high frequencies
  • Result: Only the "band" in between passes through!

Typical Implementation:

  • LC resonant circuits: Series + parallel resonances
  • Coupled resonators: Multiple circuits cascaded
  • Cavity filters: High-quality for VHF/UHF
  • Helical filters: Compact, affordable

Typical Applications:

  • Receiver preselector: Suppresses interfering signals outside band
  • TX/RX diplexer: Separates transmit/receive paths in duplexers
  • Repeater: Isolates TX and RX frequencies
  • Spectrum management: Prevents interference between bands

Importance:

  • Without filter: Receiver can overload (strong signals outside band)
  • With filter: Selectivity improved (only desired band visible)
  • Legal: Often required for licensed radio services

🎵 Expected Audio Events

Reflection Parameter (S11) - Matching

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Valley-Peak-Valley pattern:
  • 140 MHz (left): High tone (SWR 9.0 = poor match outside band)
  • 145.5 MHz (center): Low tone (SWR 1.01 = perfect match in passband!)
  • 152 MHz (right): High tone again (SWR 10.0 = rejection)
  • Valley in the center = Passband!
  • Flanks on left/right show transition to stopband

Meaning:

  • Low SWR in-band: Filter is well matched (little reflection)
  • High SWR outside: Filter reflects (energy returns = rejection)
  • Valley shape = Characteristic bandpass audio pattern!

Position in stereo field:

  • Sound begins high (poor at 140 MHz)
  • Falls downward (improving)
  • Lowest point in center (best at 145.5 MHz)
  • Rises again upward (worsening at 152 MHz)

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Valley-Peak-Valley pattern (mirror image of SWR!)
  • 140 MHz: Low tone (~2.6 dB = much reflection)
  • 145.5 MHz: Very high tone (38 dB = little reflection)
  • 152 MHz: Low tone again (2.3 dB)
  • Peak in the center!

Meaning:

  • Shows same information as SWR, presented differently
  • High return loss = Good match = Passband

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Variation around 50Ω target value
  • In passband: Near 50Ω (medium tone)
  • Outside: Deviations (higher or lower)
  • Wave-like across frequency

Meaning:

  • In-band: Filter doesn't transform (50Ω stays 50Ω)
  • Outside: Impedance deviates (filter reflects, not matched)

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth)

What you should hear:

  • Oscillation around zero line
  • In passband: Near 0Ω (purely resistive)
  • Outside: Positive and negative reactance (resonance effects)
  • Zero crossing at band center

Meaning:

  • Zero crossing = Resistive match in passband
  • Reactance outside = Filter shows resonant character

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Low phase in passband (~0° to -10°)
  • Higher phases outside
  • Relatively linear in passband (good filter design)

Meaning:

  • Linear phase in passband = Constant group delay
  • Important for digital modes

Transmission Parameter (S21) - Passband/Stopband ⭐ MOST IMPORTANT CURVES!

6. S21 Magnitude (Transmission Loss)

Activate:

  • In acoustic mode: Key 6 or Key T

What you should hear:

  • DRAMATIC peak characteristic:
  • 140 MHz (left): Very low tone (-20 dB = 10% transmission = rejection!)
  • 143-148 MHz (center): Very high tone (-0.01 to -1 dB = >89% transmission!)
  • 150+ MHz (right): Extremely low tone (-40 dB = 0.01% = almost total rejection!)
  • Steep flanks on left and right of passband peak
  • Peak at 145.5 MHz (minimal attenuation!)

Meaning:

  • High tone = Low attenuation = Passband!
  • Low tone = High attenuation = Stopband!
  • This is THE characteristic audio for bandpass!
  • Steep flanks = High filter quality (selective!)

Comparison with other scenarios:

  • Scenario 5 (Low-pass): Tone drops continuously (no right flank)
  • Scenario 10 (Bandpass): Tone rises, peaks, drops again (flanks on BOTH SIDES!)

Stereo position:

  • Sound rises from bottom to top (140→145 MHz: attenuation decreases)
  • Peak in center (145.5 MHz: Maximum transmission!)
  • Falls from top to bottom (145→152 MHz: attenuation increases)
  • Like "mountain in 3D space" → Sound goes up and back down!

7. S21 Phase (Transmission Phase)

Activate:

  • In acoustic mode: Key 7 or Key P

What you should hear:

  • Relatively linear trend in passband
  • Discontinuities at band edges (transition passband→stopband)
  • Phase changes more outside band

Meaning:

  • Linear phase in passband = Good group delay
  • Phase jumps at band edges = Normal for filters
  • Outside band: Phase less relevant (signal is blocked anyway)

Expected Smith Audio Cues

Mode 4: SWR Circles (RECOMMENDED!) ⭐

What you should hear:

  • Sound comes closer (140→145 MHz)
  • Begins far away (SWR 9.0 outside band)
  • Moves toward you (SWR decreases)
  • Sound is NEAR at 145.5 MHz (SWR 1.01 = perfect match!)
  • Sound moves away again (145→152 MHz)
  • Moves away from you (SWR increases)
  • Ends far away (SWR 10.0)

Meaning:

  • Near-far-near movement = Bandpass characteristic!
  • Near = Passband (good match)
  • Far = Stopband (poor match = reflection)
  • Unforgettable acoustic signature!

Mode 1: Cartesian

What you should hear:

  • Complex trajectory around center
  • Sound approaches center in passband
  • At center at 145.5 MHz (perfect 50Ω match)
  • Leaves center outside band

Meaning:

  • Center approach = Match improving
  • Center departure = Mismatch (filter reflects)

Mode 3: Impedance Direct

What you should hear:

  • Wave-like movement horizontally and vertically
  • In the middle (R=50Ω, X=0Ω) at 145.5 MHz
  • Oscillation around center outside

Meaning:

  • Center = Perfect impedance match in passband
  • Oscillation = Filter resonances outside band

Center Pulse (Reference Signal)

Activate:

  1. Key A in acoustic mode → Audio configuration
  2. Menu: C → Smith Audio → Activate center pulse

What you hear:

  • Periodic pulse from the CENTER
  • Sound approaches pulse (140→145 MHz)
  • Sound overlaps with pulse at 145.5 MHz (perfect!)
  • Sound moves away from pulse (145→152 MHz)
  • Approach-and-retreat pattern = Bandpass!

🔧 Exercises and Learning Tasks

Exercise 1: Bandpass vs. Low-pass Difference

Goal: Acoustically distinguish two filter types

  1. Load scenario 5 (Low-pass filter)
  • S21 magnitude: Continuously falling (no right flank)
  • Audio: Tone drops, ends low
  1. Load scenario 10 (Bandpass filter)
  • S21 magnitude: Rises, peaks, falls (BOTH flanks!)
  • Audio: Tone rises, peaks, drops again
  1. Compare:
  • Low-pass: One flank (only top)
  • Bandpass: Two flanks (top AND bottom)
  • Acoustically clearly distinguishable!

Exercise 2: Identify Passband

Goal: Acoustically locate band edges

  1. Turn on only S21 magnitude (Key 6)
  2. Press SPACEBAR
  3. Listen:
  • Where does the rise begin? (lower band edge ~143 MHz)
  • Where is the peak? (band center ~145.5 MHz)
  • Where does the descent begin? (upper band edge ~147 MHz)
  1. Press SPACEBAR (Pause) at peak
  2. Key I (Info) → Read frequency → Should be ~145.5 MHz!

Exercise 3: Evaluate Filter Quality (Steepness)

Goal: Acoustically assess flank steepness Flank steepness:

  • Steep: Attenuation changes quickly (few MHz for 10→60 dB)
  • Shallow: Attenuation changes slowly (many MHz for 10→60 dB)

Practically:

  1. S21 magnitude active
  2. Listen to the flanks (left and right of peak)
  3. Question: How fast does the tone change?
  • Fast (few seconds for high→low) = Steep flank = High quality!
  • Slow = Shallow flank = Low quality
  1. This filter: Relatively steep (good quality!)

Exercise 4: S11 vs. S21 Interpretation

Goal: Understand both parameters together

  1. Turn on only S11 (SWR) (Key 1)
  • Hear: Valley in band center (good match)
  1. Turn on only S21 (Key 6)
  • Hear: Peak in band center (good transmission)
  1. Turn on both (Key 1 + Key 6)
  • Hear simultaneously:
  • S11 low + S21 high = Passband (perfect!)
  • S11 high + S21 low = Stopband (perfect!)

Interpretation:

  • Good filter:
  • In passband: S11 low (matched) + S21 high (transmissive)
  • In stopband: S11 high (reflects) + S21 low (blocks)
  • Poor filter:
  • In passband: S11 high (not matched) OR S21 low (high insertion loss)
  • In stopband: S21 high (doesn't block enough)

Exercise 5: Y-Axis Ruler for S21

Goal: Hear absolute attenuation values

  1. Press key Y (Y-axis ruler)
  2. Key 6 (select S21 magnitude)
  3. You hear ascending scale from min (-40 dB) to max (-0.01 dB)
  4. SPACEBAR for measurement
  5. Compare:
  • Peak tone = Highest tone of scale (≈ 0 dB)
  • Flank tone = Middle tones (-10 to -30 dB)
  • Stopband tone = Lowest tone of scale (-40 dB)

Exercise 6: Smith Modes for Bandpass

Goal: Find best visualization

  1. Smith mode 4 (SWR circles): Near-far-near movement (very intuitive!)
  2. Smith mode 1 (Cartesian): Center approach (also good)
  3. Smith mode 2 (Polar): Radius variation + rotation
  4. Smith mode 3 (Impedance): Center crossing
  5. Question: Which mode shows bandpass best? → Mode 4!

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

What You Learn from this Scenario

1. Understand Bandpass Characteristics

Definition:

  • Passband: Frequencies with low attenuation
  • Stopband: Frequencies with high attenuation
  • Band edges: Transitions between passband and stopband
  • Center frequency: Frequency with minimal attenuation

2. Acoustic Signatures for Bandpass

  • S11 (SWR): Valley in band center (good match)
  • S21 magnitude: Peak in band center (good transmission)
  • Combination: Valley (S11) + Peak (S21) = Bandpass!
  • Smith SWR circles: Near-far-near movement

3. Acoustically Distinguish Filter Types

Filter Type  | S21 Audio Characteristic

-------------|--------------------------- Low-pass     | Continuously falling (one flank) High-pass    | Continuously rising (one flank) Bandpass     | Rising → Peak → Falling (two flanks!) Band-stop    | Falling → Valley → Rising (inverted bandpass)

4. S11 + S21 Interaction

In passband (desired):

  • S11 low (SWR ~1) = Good match = Little reflection
  • S21 high (≈ 0 dB) = Low loss = Signal passes through
  • Both important!

In stopband (desired):

  • S11 high (SWR >> 1) = Poor match = Reflection (rejection!)
  • S21 low (<< 0 dB) = High loss = Signal is attenuated
  • Double rejection = Very effective!

5. Practical Application

  • Receiver preselector: Improves large-signal handling
  • TX/RX separation: Enables simultaneous duplex communication
  • Spectrum management: Prevents interference
  • Multi-band TRX: Separates bands from each other

Evaluate filter quality:

  • Insertion loss: < 1 dB good, < 0.5 dB very good, < 0.1 dB excellent
  • Rejection: > 30 dB good, > 40 dB very good, > 60 dB excellent
  • Flank steepness: Important for narrow frequency spacing
  • Bandwidth: Should cover usable band, but not too wide

⚠️ Important Notes

Reality Check: Bandpass Filters in Practice

Typical Values (commercial filters):

Parameter            | Budget      | Amateur    | Professional

---------------------|-------------|------------|------------- Insertion loss       | 1-3 dB      | 0.5-1 dB   | 0.1-0.3 dB Rejection @ ±10 MHz  | 20-30 dB    | 30-40 dB   | 50-80 dB Flank steepness      | 10 dB/MHz   | 15 dB/MHz  | 20+ dB/MHz Bandwidth (-3 dB)    | Wide        | Medium     | Narrow/adjustable Price                | 10-30€      | 50-150€    | 300-2000€

Application Examples:

  • Scanner preamplifier: Bandpass 140-150 MHz, rejection >40 dB @ FM broadcast (88-108 MHz)
  • Repeater duplex: Two very narrow bandpasses (TX/RX), isolation >80 dB
  • Contest station: Bandpass per band, protects RX from strong signals of other bands

Filter Tuning

Alignment Process (cavity filter):

  1. Center frequency: Tuning screws for resonance at design frequency
  2. Bandwidth: Adjust coupling between resonators
  3. Passband: Minimal insertion loss through matching
  4. Rejection: Maximum attenuation outside through resonance quality

Tools:

  • VNA: Shows S11 + S21 (essential!)
  • Tracking generator + spectrum analyzer: Alternative
  • Dip meter: For rough pre-alignment

Practically:

  • Professional cavity filters: Often not user-adjustable (factory aligned)
  • Helical filters: Sometimes with tuning screws
  • LC filters: Often fixed dimensioned

Multiple Filters

Cascading:

  • One filter: Moderate rejection (e.g., 30 dB)
  • Two filters cascaded: Doubled rejection (e.g., 60 dB)!
  • But: Insertion loss also doubles!
