Training Scenario 6: Multiband Antenna (Multiple Resonances)

Training file: Training/06_multiband_antenne.csv Frequency range: 50.000 - 222.000 MHz (6m, 4m, 2m, 1.25m bands) Difficulty level: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Advanced (Large frequency span!) Learning objective: Recognize multiple resonances and understand multiband behavior

📋 Scenario Description

Fictional Component

You are measuring a multiband vertical antenna (e.g. Diamond V-2000 style) over a very wide frequency range. The antenna has three separate resonances at approximately 52.5 MHz (6m), 73 MHz (4m) and 146.5 MHz (2m). This is typical for trap dipoles, trap verticals or multi-element collinear arrays.

Technical Characteristics

  • Frequency ranges: 50-54 MHz (6m), 70-74 MHz (4m), 144-148 MHz (2m), 220-222 MHz (1.25m)
  • Resonance points (SWR ≈ 1.0):
  • 52.5 MHz: SWR 1.01, Z = 50.5Ω + j0.5Ω (perfect!)
  • 73.0 MHz: SWR 1.00, Z = 50.2Ω + j0.2Ω (perfect!)
  • 146.5 MHz: SWR 1.01, Z = 50.5Ω + j0.3Ω (perfect!)
  • In-between ranges: SWR up to 3.20 (poor match)
  • Behavior: Inductive → Resistive → Capacitive cyclically at each resonance
  • Special feature: Dramatic Smith chart movement over large frequency span!

Physical Significance

Multiband antennas utilize various concepts:

  1. Traps (blocking circuits): Electrically shorter antenna sections at higher frequencies
  2. Multiple resonances: Harmonic relationships (e.g. λ/2 at f₁, λ at 2×f₁)
  3. Collinear arrays: Multiple λ/2 radiators in series (2m band)
  4. Matching networks: Internally matched for multiple bands

Typical Applications:

  • VHF/UHF multiband verticals (scanners, amateur radio)
  • HF trap dipoles (e.g. 80/40/20m)
  • Discone antennas (ultra-wideband)
  • Mobile phone antennas (multi-frequency systems)

Advantages:

  • One antenna for multiple bands
  • Space saving (important for balcony stations!)
  • Cost savings

Disadvantages:

  • Compromises in gain and bandwidth
  • More complex tuning
  • Between bands: Poor matching

🎵 Expected Audio Events

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave) ⭐ VERY DYNAMIC!

What you should hear:

  • THREE distinct valleys (low tones) at resonance frequencies:
  1. 50-54 MHz (6m band): Low at 52.5 MHz (SWR 1.01)
  2. 70-74 MHz (4m band): Low at 73 MHz (SWR 1.00)
  3. 144-148 MHz (2m band): Low at 146.5 MHz (SWR 1.01)
  • HIGH peaks between resonances (SWR up to 3.20)
  • Rollercoaster ride throughout entire sweep!
  • Extremely dynamic pitch progression

Stereo Position:

  • Sound moves dramatically in the stereo field
  • Left (50 MHz): High → Low → High
  • Center (70-144 MHz): High → Low → High
  • Right (220 MHz): Rising again

Significance:

  • Three frequencies with perfect matching
  • In between: Mismatch (antenna not resonant)
  • Classic multiband behavior
  • Acoustically unforgettable! - sounds like "waves in the ocean"

Comparison with other scenarios:

  • Scenario 1: Flat line (single-band, perfect)
  • Scenario 2-3: Monotonic trend (too short/long)
  • Scenario 6: MULTIPLE PEAKS AND VALLEYS (multiband!)

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • THREE peaks (very high tones) at resonances:
  • 52.5 MHz: ~38 dB
  • 73.0 MHz: ~54 dB
  • 146.5 MHz: ~38 dB
  • Valleys (low tones) between bands (~4-9 dB)
  • Mirror image of the SWR curve!
  • Extremely high-contrast progression

Significance:

  • High return loss at resonances = Little reflection
  • Low return loss between bands = Much reflection
  • Shows same information as SWR, different format

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Oscillating progression between low-impedance and high-impedance
  • At resonances: Medium tone (~50Ω)
  • Between resonances: Fluctuates between ~40Ω and ~85Ω
  • Wave movement throughout entire sweep
  • Characteristic triangle wave sound with strong dynamics

Significance:

  • Impedance fluctuates around 50Ω target value
  • At resonance: Hits 50Ω exactly
  • Between resonances: Deviates (too low or too high)
  • Shows tuning quality of multiband matching

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth) ⚡ VERY IMPORTANT!

What you should hear:

  • THREE zero crossings (tone around center):
  • 52.5 MHz: X ≈ 0Ω (resistive)
  • 73.0 MHz: X ≈ 0Ω (resistive)
  • 146.5 MHz: X ≈ 0Ω (resistive)
  • Between zero crossings:
  • Upward movement: Inductive (X positive)
  • Downward movement: Capacitive (X negative)
  • Sawtooth wave pattern: Up → Zero → Down → Zero → Up (cyclical!)

Significance:

  • Zero crossing = Resonance (purely resistive)
  • Upward to resonance = Antenna too short (inductive)
  • Downward from resonance = Antenna too long (capacitive)
  • Classic antenna behavior at EACH resonance!

Acoustic Pattern:

  • Sounds like "waves on the beach" in sawtooth sound
  • High → Down → High → Down (cyclical)
  • Three cycles over frequency range

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Fluctuating phase around 0° at resonances
  • Positive phases (inductive) and negative phases (capacitive)
  • Passages through 0° at each resonance
  • Correlates with reactance curve

Significance:

  • Phase ≈ 0° = Resonance
  • Positive phase = Inductive
  • Negative phase = Capacitive
  • Shows same information as reactance, presented differently

🎯 Smith Chart Audio Visualization

Activate Smith Visualization

  2. Press key B to select mode:

Expected Smith Audio Cues

Mode 4: SWR Circles (RECOMMENDED!) ⭐

What you should hear:

  • Sound moves UP AND DOWN in space:
  • Close to you (50 MHz resonance): Low SWR
  • Far away (between bands): High SWR
  • Close again (73 MHz resonance): Low SWR
  • Far away (between bands)
  • Close again (146.5 MHz resonance): Low SWR
  • Further away again (220 MHz)
  • THREE approaches to you = Three resonances!
  • Like waves coming towards you and going away again

Significance:

  • Close = Low SWR = Resonance = Good match
  • Far away = High SWR = No resonance = Poor match
  • Perfect visualization for multiband character!

Audio Experience:

  • Like "tide" - sound comes closer, goes away, comes back
  • Three waves over the sweep
  • Unforgettable acoustic signature!

Mode 1: Cartesian

What you should hear:

  • Complex trajectory through Smith space
  • Sound moves crosswise:
  • Starts bottom right (inductive, low-impedance)
  • Moves to center (first resonance)
  • Moves top left (capacitive, high-impedance)
  • Back to center (second resonance)
  • Again crosswise to third resonance
  • Loop-like movement possible

Significance:

  • Center = Resonance (50Ω + j0)
  • Movement away from center = Mismatch
  • Complex paths = Multiple resonances

Mode 2: Polar/Radius

What you should hear:

  • Rotation around center combined with radius change
  • Radius shrinks at resonances (close to center)
  • Radius grows between resonances (far from center)
  • Three shrink-grow cycles

Significance:

  • Radius = |Γ| (reflection coefficient magnitude)
  • Small radius = Little reflection = Resonance
  • Large radius = Much reflection = Mismatch

Mode 3: Impedance Direct

What you should hear:

  • Horizontal oscillation (R fluctuates)
  • Vertical oscillation (X fluctuates)
  • Sound crosses center three times
  • Like 3D Lissajous figure

Significance:

  • Center crossings = Resonances (R=50Ω, X=0Ω)
  • Oscillation shows impedance variation across bands

Center Pulse (Reference Signal)

Activate:

  1. Press key A in acoustic mode → Audio Configuration
  2. Menu: C → Smith Audio → Enable Center Pulse

What you hear:

  • Periodic pulse from the CENTER (reference = 50Ω + j0)
  • Your measurement crosses the pulse THREE TIMES!
  • At each crossing = One resonance reached
  • Between crossings: Sound moves away from pulse

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

What You Learn from this Scenario

1. Understand Multiband Antenna Concept

  • Multiple resonances through traps, harmonic lengths or matching networks
  • Between bands: Poor matching (normal!)
  • On design bands: Good matching
  • Compromise: Multiple bands vs. performance per band

2. Acoustic Signatures for Multiband

  • SWR: Multiple valleys (minima) = Multiple resonances
  • Reactance X: Multiple zero crossings = Resonance points
  • Smith SWR Circles: Sound comes close multiple times = Multiple matches
  • Up-and-down pattern is characteristic!

3. Bandwidth Concept

  • Each resonance has its own bandwidth
  • Multiband ≠ Wideband!
  • Wideband: Continuously good over large range (e.g. Discone)
  • Multiband: Good on multiple specific bands

4. Trap Antenna Theory

How do traps work?

  • Trap = Parallel resonant circuit at blocking frequency
  • At resonance: Very high impedance = electrical open circuit
  • Low frequency: Trap conducts → Long antenna
  • High frequency: Trap blocks → Short antenna (rest isolated)

Example:

[←────────────── 40m element ──────────────→]

[←──── 20m element ────→][TRAP][Isolated]  At 7 MHz:   Trap conducts  → Full length (40m) At 14 MHz:  Trap blocks    → Only 20m part active

5. Practical Relevance

  • Mobile/Portable: One antenna instead of several → Space advantage!
  • Balcony stations: Often only room for one antenna
  • Scanner application: Listen to many bands simultaneously
  • Amateur radio: One mast instead of three

⚠️ Important Notes

Reality Check: Multiband Antennas in Practice

Typical Multiband Antennas:

  1. VHF/UHF verticals: 2m/70cm (most common combination)
  2. HF trap dipoles: 80/40/20m or 40/20/10m
  3. Fan dipoles: Multiple wires for different bands
  4. End-fed half-wave: Multi-band through harmonic resonances
  5. Discone: Ultra-wideband (50-1300 MHz), but lower gain

Performance Compromises:

  • Gain: Usually 2-3 dB less than dedicated mono-band antenna
  • Bandwidth: Per band narrower than mono-band
  • Efficiency: Traps cause losses (0.5-1 dB typical)
  • Size: Still quite large (defined by lowest band)

When Multiband, When Mono-Band?

  • Multiband: Space problems, budget, aesthetics, portable
  • Mono-Band: Maximum performance, contest, DXing, fixed location with space

Measurements on Multiband Antennas

Important Points:

  1. Wide frequency sweep: Must cover ALL design bands!
  2. Fine resolution: Resonances can be narrow
  3. Between bands: Poor values are normal (don't fix!)
  4. Tuning: Tune each resonance individually (complex!)

NanoVNA Settings for Multiband:

Start: Lowest band - 5%

Stop:  Highest band + 5% Points: 201 or more (fine resolution!)  Example for 2m/70cm: Start: 140 MHz Stop:  450 MHz Points: 201

🔬 Deep Dive: Harmonic Resonances

Why Multiple Resonances?

Harmonic Theory:

An antenna can be resonant at multiple frequencies:

Dipole length L = λ/2 at fundamental frequency f₀  At 2×f₀: L = λ (full wave) → Also resonant! At 3×f₀: L = 1.5λ            → Also resonant!  Example: 50 MHz: λ/2 = 3m → Resonance 100 MHz: λ = 3m  → Resonance 150 MHz: 1.5λ = 3m → Resonance

BUT:

  • Impedance changes!
  • λ/2: ~70Ω (dipole-typical)
  • λ: Very high impedance (>1000Ω) → Poor match
  • Traps or matching networks needed for practical use

This Scenario:

  • Probably uses traps + matching networks
  • All three resonances have ~50Ω
  • Engineered Multiband (not just harmonic)
