Training Scenario 7: Coaxial Cable with Attenuation (S21 Analysis)

Training File: Training/07_kabel_mit_daempfung.csv Frequency Range: 144.000 - 146.000 MHz (2m amateur radio band) Difficulty Level: ⭐⭐ Medium Learning Objective: Understanding cable attenuation through S21 measurement and recognizing frequency-dependent losses

📋 Scenario Description

Fictional Component

You are measuring a 20 meter long RG-58 coaxial cable across the 2m band. The cable has a perfectly matched 50Ω load at the end (simulating perfect antenna). The cable itself shows typical attenuation characteristics: Perfect matching (S11), but measurable loss (S21).

Technical Properties

  • Cable Length: 20 meters
  • Cable Type: RG-58 (5mm diameter, solid PE)
  • Impedance (S11): 50.1Ω + j0.1Ω (constant, perfect!)
  • SWR: 1.00 (ideal across entire band!)
  • Return Loss (S11): 54.10 dB (virtually no reflection)
  • Transmission (S21):
  • 144.0 MHz: -0.005 dB (99.9% = ~5W loss at 100W)
  • 146.0 MHz: -0.015 dB (99.7% = ~15W loss at 100W)
  • Special Feature: S21 becomes worse with increasing frequency (typical for cables!)

Physical Meaning

Coaxial cable losses arise from:

  1. Dielectric losses: RF energy heats insulation (PE, PTFE)
  2. Ohmic losses: Resistance of center conductor and braid
  3. Skin effect: Current flows only in surface (frequency-dependent!)
  4. Radiation losses: Minimal RF radiation through braid gaps

Frequency Dependence:

  • Attenuation increases with √f (rough approximation)
  • Higher frequencies: More attenuated
  • Skin effect: Resistance increases with frequency

Typical Attenuation Values (per 100m):

Cable Type  | 144 MHz | 432 MHz | 1296 MHz

------------|---------|---------|---------- RG-58       | 3.5 dB  | 6.5 dB  | 12 dB RG-213      | 2.0 dB  | 3.8 dB  | 7.5 dB Aircom+     | 0.9 dB  | 1.7 dB  | 3.2 dB Ecoflex 10  | 0.5 dB  | 1.0 dB  | 2.0 dB

Practical Significance:

  • At UHF/SHF: Cable selection critical!
  • At HF/VHF: Even RG-58 acceptable (short lengths)
  • Rule of thumb: Maximum cable length at <3 dB loss

🎵 Expected Audio Events

Reflection Parameters (S11) - Cable Matching

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave)

What You Should Hear:

  • Extremely quiet, very low constant tone (SWR = 1.00)
  • Absolutely constant across entire band
  • No variation perceptible
  • Identical to Scenario 1 (perfect resonance)

Meaning:

  • Cable is perfectly terminated (50Ω load at end)
  • No reflections from cable end
  • SWR=1.0 does NOT mean "no loss"! (common mistake)
  • S11 says nothing about losses! → Need S21!

Position in Stereo Field:

  • Sound remains exactly centered
  • No movement

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What You Should Hear:

  • Very high, constant tone (54 dB)
  • Minimally audible "flutter" (numerical noise)
  • Virtually no variation

Meaning:

  • Extremely high return loss = Virtually no reflection
  • Cable behaves like ideal 50Ω load
  • BUT: Says nothing about transmission!

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What You Should Hear:

  • Constant medium tone exactly at 50Ω
  • Minimally audible "tremor" around 50.1Ω
  • No perceptible pitch change

Meaning:

  • Cable does not transform impedance
  • Load impedance (50Ω) appears unchanged at cable input
  • Perfect behavior for 50Ω system

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth)

What You Should Hear:

  • Extremely quiet tone around zero line (±0.1Ω)
  • Tone "hovers" around center
  • Practically inaudible

Meaning:

  • No reactance = Purely resistive
  • Cable is resonance-free (no line resonances in measurement range)
  • 50Ω termination works perfectly

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What You Should Hear:

  • Very quiet tone near zero point
  • Minimal fluctuations

Meaning:

  • Zero phase = Resistive load
  • Cable behaves like resistor (from S11 perspective)

Transmission Parameters (S21) - Cable Losses ⭐ IMPORTANT!

6. S21 Magnitude (Transmission Loss)

Activate:

  • In acoustic mode: Key 6 or Key T

What You Should Hear:

  • Very high, but slowly descending tone
  • Starts at extremely high level (-0.005 dB at 144 MHz)
  • Drops slightly over frequency
  • Ends at still high, but lower tone (-0.015 dB at 146 MHz)
  • Continuous downward trend (lowpass-like)

Meaning:

  • S21 = Transmission (how much signal gets through cable?)
  • High tone (close to 0 dB) = Low attenuation = Little loss
  • Decreasing with frequency = Typical cable characteristic!
  • Higher frequency → More loss (skin effect + dielectric losses)

Loss Calculation:

At 144 MHz: -0.005 dB

→ Transmission = 10^(-0.005/20) = 0.9994 = 99.94% → Loss = 0.06% ≈ 60 mW at 100W  At 146 MHz: -0.015 dB → Transmission = 10^(-0.015/20) = 0.9983 = 99.83% → Loss = 0.17% ≈ 170 mW at 100W

Comparison with Scenario 5 (Lowpass Filter):

  • Filter: S21 drops more steeply (intentional - attenuate harmonics)
  • Cable: S21 drops minimally (undesired - loss!)
  • Both: Lowpass characteristic, but different magnitude

Stereo Position:

  • Sound moves slightly downward (attenuation increases)
  • Shows frequency dependence spatially

7. S21 Phase (Transmission Phase)

Activate:

  • In acoustic mode: Key 7 or Key P

What You Should Hear:

  • Strongly descending tone (phase becomes strongly negative)
  • Starts at approximately -1° to -2°
  • Drops to approximately -50° to -60° at 146 MHz
  • Linear downward trend

Meaning:

  • S21 Phase = Propagation delay through cable
  • Negative phase = Signal takes time through cable (normal!)
  • Linear phase = Constant Group Delay = Good!
  • Group Delay τ = Δφ / Δω (slope of phase curve)

Propagation Time Calculation:

Cable length: 20m

Velocity factor (RG-58): v = 0.66 Signal speed: c × 0.66 = 198,000 km/s  Propagation time: t = 20m / (198,000 km/s) = 101 ns  Phase shift at 145 MHz: φ = 360° × 145 MHz × 101 ns = 5270° = 14.6 full circles + 230°  In Smith chart: 14× full rotation + partial rotation

Practical Significance:

  • Linear phase = No signal distortion
  • Important for digital modes (RTTY, FT8, etc.)
  • SSB telephony: Usually not critical

🔧 Exercises and Learning Tasks

Exercise 1: S11 vs. S21 - Critical Difference!

Goal: Understand that SWR=1.0 does NOT mean "lossless"

  1. Turn off all curves
  2. Turn on only SWR (Key 1)
  • SPACE → Hear: Perfect! (SWR=1.0)
  • Conclusion (WRONG): "Cable is perfect, no loss!"
  1. Turn off SWR
  2. Turn on only S21 Magnitude (Key 6)
  • SPACE → Hear: Slightly descending (-0.005 to -0.015 dB)
  • Conclusion (CORRECT): "Cable has measurable losses!"

Important Insight:

  • S11 (SWR): Shows only reflection/matching
  • S21 (Transmission): Shows actual insertion loss
  • Perfect matching (SWR=1.0) can have high losses!
  • Example: 50Ω attenuator: SWR=1.0, but S21=-10dB or more!

Exercise 2: Frequency Dependence of Attenuation

Goal: Understand that higher frequencies are attenuated more

  1. Turn on only S21 Magnitude (Key 6)
  2. Press SPACE
  3. Focus on pitch trend
  4. Question: Does tone go higher or lower? → Lower! (More attenuation)
  5. Interpretation:
  • 144 MHz (left): Highest tone = Lowest attenuation
  • 146 MHz (right): Lower tone = Higher attenuation
  • Trend: Higher frequency → More loss

Exercise 3: Estimate Cable Length from S21 Phase

Goal: Understand group delay and cable length

  1. Turn on only S21 Phase (Key 7)
  2. Press SPACE
  3. Hear the strong downward trend
  4. Interpretation:
  • Strong phase gradient = Long propagation time = Long cable
  • Vs. Scenario 5 (filter): Less steep phase = Short element
  1. Rough calculation:
  • Phase change ~50° over 2 MHz
  • At 145 MHz: 360° = λ = 2.07m (electrical)
  • 50° ≈ 0.14× λ ≈ 0.29m per 2 MHz
  • But: Cable makes many full rotations → Total phase >> 360°!

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

What You Learn from this Scenario

1. Critical Difference S11 vs. S21

  • S11 = Reflection: Shows matching (SWR, Return Loss)
  • S21 = Transmission: Shows insertion loss
  • Important: Perfect matching ≠ Lossless!
  • Both parameters are essential for complete assessment

2. Cable Attenuation Characteristics

  • Frequency-dependent: Higher frequency → More attenuation
  • Length-dependent: Double length ≈ Double attenuation (dB)
  • Cable-type dependent: Thicker/better = Less attenuation
  • Approximation: Attenuation ∝ √f (roughly)

3. Acoustic Signatures for Cables

  • S11 (SWR): Perfectly constant (when well terminated)
  • S21 Magnitude: Slightly descending with frequency
  • S21 Phase: Strongly linear descending (Group Delay)
  • Smith (S11): Central (when terminated), rotating (electrical length)

4. Group Delay and Propagation Time

  • Phase slope = Group Delay = Signal propagation time
  • Linear phase = Constant Group Delay = Good
  • Non-linear phase = Frequency-dependent Delay = Dispersion (bad)
  • Coax cables usually have very good Group Delay (linear)

5. Practical Reference

  • Cable Selection:
  • VHF: RG-58 OK for <30m, RG-213 for longer
  • UHF: RG-213 or Aircom+, not RG-58!
  • SHF (>1 GHz): Only high-quality cables (Ecoflex, LMR, Aircom)
  • Length Minimization: Shortest path transmitter → antenna
  • Connector Quality: Poor connectors = More loss than cable!

  • Microwave (>2 GHz): Only premium cables (Ecoflex, LMR-400+)

Cable Measurement with VNA

Best Practices:

  1. Calibration: At cable end (if possible), not at VNA!
  2. Load: High-quality 50Ω termination (not open connector!)
  3. Frequency Range: Measure widely (shows frequency dependence)
  4. S11 + S21: Measure both parameters for complete characterization

Typical Problems:

  • Poor Termination: S11 shows reflections (SWR > 1.1)
  • Poor Connectors: High S21 attenuation (more than cable spec)
  • Cable Damage: Jumps in S11/S21 (kink, water ingress)

Cable Aging

Aging Effects:

  1. Oxidation: Braid/center conductor oxidizes → Higher resistance
  2. Water Ingress: Moist dielectric → Higher losses
  3. UV Damage: Outer jacket becomes brittle → Water ingress
  4. Mechanical Damage: Kinks, crushing → Impedance discontinuities

Lifespan:

  • Indoor: 20-30 years (if dry)
  • Outdoor without UV protection: 5-10 years
  • Outdoor with UV protection: 10-20 years
  • Marine/Salt: 3-5 years (aggressive!)

Maintenance:

  • Annually: S11 sweep (VNA) for condition monitoring
  • When abnormalities: S21 measurement (attenuation increased?)
  • Prevention: Connector sealing (self-amalgamating tape)

🔬 Deep Dive: Skin Effect and Attenuation

Why Does Attenuation Increase with Frequency?

Skin Effect:

At DC: Current flows uniformly through entire conductor

At RF: Current flows only in "skin" of conductor  Skin depth δ = √(ρ / (π × f × μ)) ρ = specific resistance (Copper: 1.7×10⁻⁸ Ωm) f = frequency μ = permeability  At 145 MHz (Copper): δ ≈ 5.5 μm (only 0.0055 mm!) → Effective conductor cross-section shrinks → Resistance increases → Attenuation increases

Dielectric Losses:

  • RF field in dielectric (PE, PTFE)
  • Molecular friction → Heat
  • Loss factor tan(δ): PE ≈ 0.0002, PTFE ≈ 0.0001
  • Increases with frequency

Total Attenuation:

α (dB/m) ≈ α_Conductor + α_Dielectric

α_Conductor ∝ √f  (skin effect) α_Dielectric ∝ f  (dielectric losses)  At VHF: α_Conductor dominates At UHF/SHF: Both significant At microwave: α_Dielectric dominates
