Training Scenario 2: Antenna Too Short (Inductive)

Training file: Training/02_antenne_zu_kurz_induktiv.csv Frequency range: 144.000 - 146.000 MHz (2m amateur radio band) Difficulty level: ⭐⭐ Medium (Advanced beginners) Learning objective: Understand how inductive mismatch sounds and is recognized

📋 Scenario Description

Fictitious Component

You are measuring a 2m Yagi antenna that is too short. The radiator is approximately 3-5 cm shorter than optimal. This leads to inductive behavior - the antenna acts like a coil with a resistor in series.

Technical Properties (at 144 MHz)

  • Impedance: 35.2Ω + j28.5Ω (inductive!)
  • SWR: 1.95 (acceptable, but not optimal)
  • Return Loss: 8.54 dB (moderate)
  • Reactance (X): +28.5Ω at 144 MHz → decreasing → +0.5Ω at 146 MHz
  • Resistance (R): 35Ω at 144 MHz → increasing → 53Ω at 146 MHz
  • Physical behavior: Antenna becomes "electrically longer" with increasing frequency

Physical Meaning

Positive Reactance (X > 0) = Inductive:

  • Antenna too short = Not enough "electrical length"
  • Current lags voltage (like a coil)
  • With increasing frequency, antenna becomes electrically longer → Reactance decreases
  • At approx. 146 MHz, resonance would be reached (X ≈ 0)

Typical Causes:

  • Radiator mechanically cut too short
  • Incorrect frequency calculation
  • Mechanical deformation
  • Installation at too great a height (capacitive grounding missing)

🎵 Expected Audio Events

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • High tone at 144 MHz (SWR 1.95)
  • Tone becomes continuously lower over frequency
  • Clear downward trend from left to right
  • At 146 MHz: Significantly lower tone (SWR 1.01)

Stereo Position:

  • Left (144 MHz): Clearly audible, higher pitch
  • Right (146 MHz): Much quieter, low pitch
  • Movement: Clear downward (improving)

Meaning:

  • SWR decreases with increasing frequency
  • Resonance frequency lies above 146 MHz
  • Antenna becomes better matched at higher frequencies
  • Diagnosis: Antenna too short → must be lengthened

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2)

What you should hear:

  • Low tone at 144 MHz (8.54 dB = poor match)
  • Tone continuously rises over frequency
  • Strong upward trend from left to right
  • At 146 MHz: Very high tone (120 dB = excellent)

Meaning:

  • Return Loss increases = Less reflection at higher frequencies
  • Correlates with SWR improvement
  • Shows same information from different perspective

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Low tone at 144 MHz (35Ω)
  • Tone slightly rises over frequency
  • At 146 MHz: Medium tone (53Ω)
  • Upward movement, but not dramatic

Meaning:

  • Impedance magnitude increases from 35Ω → 53Ω
  • Crosses 50Ω mark somewhere in the band
  • Shows "pass-through behavior" through system impedance

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth) ⚡ MOST IMPORTANT CURVE!

What you should hear:

  • Clearly audible, falling sawtooth tone
  • Strong downward trend from left to right
  • Left (144 MHz): High tone (+28.5Ω = strongly inductive)
  • Right (146 MHz): Low tone, near zero (+0.5Ω = almost purely resistive)
  • Clear, continuous downward movement

Stereo Perception:

  • Left: Sawtooth rises (inductive)
  • Middle: Sawtooth approaches zero
  • Right: Sawtooth near zero line

Meaning: ⭐ KEY INFORMATION!

  • Positive reactance = Inductive = Antenna too short
  • Falling reactance = Antenna becomes "longer" at higher frequency
  • Zero crossing outside the band = Resonance at > 146 MHz
  • Diagnosis clear: Lengthen radiator!

5. Phase (Key 5)

What you should hear:

  • Positive phase at 144 MHz (approx. +38°)
  • Phase decreases over frequency
  • Approaches 0° at 146 MHz

Meaning:

  • Positive phase = Current lags (inductive)
  • Decreasing phase = Approach to resistive behavior

🎯 Smith Chart Audio Visualization

Mode 1: Cartesian (Recommended for Diagnosis)

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts FAR FORWARD and LEFT of you
  • Continuous movement toward BACK-RIGHT
  • Sound travels through space as frequency increases
  • At the end: Almost central, but still slightly forward

Position Meaning:

  • Forward = Im(Γ) > 0 = Positive reactance = Inductive
  • Left = Re(Γ) < 0 = Resistance below 50Ω
  • Movement backward = Reactance decreases
  • Movement to the right = Resistance increases

Acoustic Landmarks:

  1. Start (144 MHz): Clearly forward-left → "Too short, too low impedance"
  2. Middle (145 MHz): Moving toward central → "Improving"
  3. End (146 MHz): Almost central, slightly forward → "Soon optimal"

Hearing Axis Crossings:

  • When sound crosses the horizontal axis (left-right):
  • Audible: Pitched sweep up or down
  • Meaning: R = 50Ω reached (resistance matches!)
  • When sound crosses the vertical axis (forward-back):
  • Audible: Pitched sweep
  • Meaning: X = 0Ω, purely resistive (resonance!)

Expectation in this scenario:

  • Horizontal axis is crossed (R passes through 50Ω)
  • Vertical axis is NOT crossed (X remains positive, but becomes smaller)

Mode 2: Polar (For Advanced)

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts in front of you, but offset to the left
  • Sound "wanders" counterclockwise slightly to the left
  • Simultaneously: Sound becomes quieter (closer to you)
  • Distance decreases = SWR improves

Rotation Meaning:

  • Angle ∠Γ: Starts at approx. +147° (forward-left)
  • Angle becomes larger (toward ±180°, i.e., back-left)
  • Shows phase change of reflection coefficient

Radius Meaning:

  • |Γ|: Starts at approx. 0.32 (moderately far from center)
  • Radius decreases to approx. 0.01 (very close to center)
  • Audible: Sound becomes quieter, approaches you

Interpretation:

  • Small circular movement counterclockwise = Typical for inductive detuning
  • Approach to center = Improvement with frequency
  • Would reach center at resonance frequency (> 146 MHz)

Mode 3: Impedance Direct (Simplest Interpretation)

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts LEFT and FAR FORWARD
  • Movement to the RIGHT (R increases from 35Ω → 53Ω)
  • Movement toward BACK-CENTRAL (X decreases from +28.5Ω → +0.5Ω)
  • Target center (50Ω, 0Ω) is almost reached

Position Mapping:

  • Left: R < 50Ω (low impedance)
  • Center: R = 50Ω (matching)
  • Right: R > 50Ω (high impedance)
  • Forward: X > 0 (inductive)
  • Center: X = 0 (resistive)
  • Back: X < 0 (capacitive)

Diagnosis:

  • Start left-forward = Low impedance and inductive
  • Movement toward right-central = R and X improve
  • Almost center reached at 146 MHz

Mode 4: SWR Circles (Goal-Oriented)

What you should hear:

  • Sound starts FAR AWAY from you
  • Continuous approach to you
  • Volume decreases with better match
  • At the end: Sound very CLOSE to you (low SWR)

Meaning:

  • Distance = SWR level
  • Far away = SWR 1.95 (at 144 MHz)
  • Close to you = SWR 1.01 (at 146 MHz)
  • Shows match quality in an intuitive way

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

Acoustic Signatures of "Too Short / Inductive"

1. Reactance Audio Pattern

  • Falling sawtooth = Reactance decreases = Gets better at higher frequency
  • Starts high = Clearly positive (inductive)
  • Ends low = Near zero (almost resistive)
  • Diagnosis rule: Falling reactance + Positive values = Too short!

2. SWR Audio Pattern

  • High tone, falling = SWR high, gets better
  • Minimum at upper band edge = Resonance above it
  • Diagnosis rule: SWR falls to the right = Lengthen!

3. Smith Audio Pattern (Cartesian)

  • Starts forward = Positive reactance (inductive)
  • Moves backward = Reactance decreases
  • Diagonal movement toward back-right = Typical for antenna too short
  • Diagnosis rule: Forward position + Movement backward = Inductive!

4. Smith Audio Pattern (Polar)

  • Slight rotation counterclockwise = Phase change
  • Approach to center = SWR improves
  • Diagnosis rule: Counterclockwise + Closer = Inductive, gets better

Physical Understanding

Why is an antenna that's too short inductive?

  1. Electrical length < λ/4: Antenna is electrically too short
  2. Current maximum shifted: Not located at feed point
  3. Reactance dominates: Reactive impedance predominates
  4. Analogy to coil: Incomplete resonance structure acts inductively

Why does it get better at higher frequency?

  1. λ becomes smaller: At higher frequency, wavelength is shorter
  2. Antenna becomes "electrically longer": Fixed length / smaller λ = larger fraction of λ
  3. Approaches λ/4: At even higher frequency, λ/4 would be reached
  4. Resonance above it: Extrapolation shows resonance at approx. 146.5 - 147 MHz

Corrective Measures

1. Mechanical lengthening (Best solution):

  • Lengthen radiator by 3-5 cm
  • Resonance frequency drops into band
  • Broadband matching is achieved

2. Series capacitor (Electrical solution):

  • 18-20 pF in series to feed point
  • Compensates inductive reactance
  • Disadvantage: Narrowband, additional component

3. Matching network:

  • L-C network for transformation
  • Complex, not recommended for antennas
  • Better: Mechanical correction

⚠️ Important Notes

Typical Measurement Practice

Such measurements show:

  • Antenna was designed for higher frequency
  • Or: Mechanical error during construction
  • Or: Antenna for another band (e.g., 70cm) measured on 2m

Common in practice with:

  • Self-built antennas (length calculation incorrect)
  • Commercial antennas after storm (mechanical deformation)
  • Antennas in new environment (capacitive detuning)

Return Loss and SWR Relation

At 144 MHz:

  • SWR = 1.95 corresponds to Return Loss = 8.54 dB
  • This means: ~15% of power is reflected
  • 85% reaches the antenna - acceptable, but losses measurable

Comparison with Scenario 1:

  • Scenario 1: < 0.5% reflection (SWR 1.0, RL > 46 dB)
  • Scenario 2: ~15% reflection (SWR 1.95, RL 8.54 dB)
  • 30× more reflection!

Practical Tolerance

Is SWR 1.95 usable?

  • Yes! Acceptable for most applications
  • Modern transceivers: Work up to SWR 3:1 without problems
  • Antenna tuner would match without problems
  • But: Optimization is worthwhile for maximum efficiency

🔊 Audio Tips for Inductive Recognition

Key Audio Cues

  1. Reactance sawtooth falls = Inductive
  2. Smith forward, moves backward = Inductive
  3. SWR decreases to the right = Resonance above band
  4. Phase positive, decreasing = Inductive phase

Avoid Confusion

Inductive (Too short) vs. Capacitive (Too long): Inductive (This scenario):

  • Reactance: Positive, falling
  • Smith: Forward, moves backward
  • Sawtooth: Falls from high to low

Capacitive (Scenario 3):

  • Reactance: Negative, rising
  • Smith: Back, moves forward
  • Sawtooth: Rises from low to high

Mnemonic:

  • Forward = Ahead = Inductive = Coil = Too short
  • Back = Behind = Capacitive = Capacitor = Too long
