Training Scenario 9: Open Line at 5m (TDR Analysis)

Training File: Training/09_offene_leitung_5m.csv Frequency Range: 144.000 - 147.000 MHz (2m Amateur Radio Band + extended) Difficulty Level: ⭐⭐⭐ Advanced (Extreme Mismatch) Learning Objective: Recognize open circuit faults, understand difference from short circuit, TDR application

📋 Scenario Description

Fictitious Component

You are measuring a 5 meter long coaxial cable with open end (open circuit). This simulates another typical cable fault: inner conductor or antenna is broken (e.g., due to corrosion, mechanical break, or loose connection). This fault must also be quickly localized!

Technical Characteristics

  • Cable Length to Fault: 5 meters
  • Fault Type: Open Circuit (R → ∞)
  • SWR: 11.70 to 99.00 (extremely high, similar to short circuit!)
  • Return Loss: 0.17 to 3.32 dB (very poor)
  • Impedance: 1500Ω to 5000Ω (high impedance!) + strongly positive (X: +37Ω to +95Ω)
  • Reflection Coefficient: |Γ| ≈ 0.98 (almost complete reflection!)
  • Special Feature: Opposite of short circuit - Smith position right instead of left!

Physical Meaning

Open Circuit in RF Engineering:

  • Inner conductor not connected → Resistance → ∞
  • Almost complete reflection of RF energy (like short circuit)
  • Standing waves in cable (maximum SWR!)
  • Line Transformation: Z_Open = -j×Z₀×cot(βl)
  • At λ/4 intervals: Impedance transforms between ∞ and 0Ω
  • Inductive: X positive (typical for open circuit)

Typical Causes:

  • Corrosion: Inner conductor corroded through
  • Mechanical Break: Cable kinked or overstretched
  • Loose Connection: Connector not properly assembled
  • Antenna Element Broken: Radiator mechanically damaged
  • Oxidation: Contact point oxidized (high contact resistance)

Practical Significance:

  • Total Failure: No RF signal reaches antenna (like short circuit)
  • Transmitter Load: High reflection (PA protection should activate)
  • Diagnosis: VNA + TDR locates break
  • Repair: Repair conductor, reassemble connector, replace antenna element

🎧 Acoustic Analysis

Import and Load File

  1. Main Menu: Press L key (Load)
  2. File list appears - navigate with Arrow Keys
  3. Select 09_offene_leitung_5m.csv
  4. Press Enter to load
  5. Confirmation: "51 measurement points successfully loaded"
  6. Attention: Extreme values like Scenario 8!

Start Acoustic Mode

  1. Main Menu: Press A key (Acoustic Analysis)
  2. You are now in acoustic analysis mode
  3. Press SPACEBAR to start playback
  4. WARNING: Very high tones (SWR), reduce volume if necessary!

🎵 Expected Audio Events

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave) ⚠️ EXTREME!

What you should hear:

  • VERY HIGH TONE at 144 MHz (SWR 99:1!)
  • Identical with Scenario 8 (short circuit)!
  • Tone drops continuously over frequency
  • At 147 MHz: Still very high (SWR ~8:1)

Meaning:

  • SWR curve alone does NOT distinguish between short circuit and open circuit!
  • Both show extreme mismatch
  • Need other parameters (impedance, reactance) for distinction

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • VERY LOW TONE (0.17 dB at 144 MHz)
  • Also identical with Scenario 8
  • Rises slightly over frequency

Meaning:

  • Return Loss also does not distinguish between short circuit/open circuit
  • Both: Almost 100% reflection

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave) ⭐ DIFFERENCE!

What you should hear:

  • VERY HIGH TONE (1500Ω to 5000Ω)
  • Opposite of Scenario 8! (there: Very low tone)
  • Remains in extremely high impedance range
  • Far above 50Ω system impedance

Meaning:

  • High impedance = Open circuit characteristic!
  • Should ideally be ∞ (line transformation prevents exactly ∞)
  • Far from 50Ω → Massive mismatch
  • 5m cable transforms ∞ into high, but not infinite impedance
  • FIRST AUDIBLE DIFFERENCE from short circuit!

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth) ⭐ IMPORTANT!

What you should hear:

  • STRONGLY POSITIVE TONE (strongly inductive!)
  • X values: +37Ω to +95Ω (very positive!)
  • Opposite of Scenario 8! (there: Strongly negative)
  • Tone rises over frequency (becomes more inductive)

Meaning:

  • Positive reactance = Inductive
  • Open circuit typically shows inductive behavior
  • Combination with high R = Open circuit identification!
  • SECOND AUDIBLE DIFFERENCE from short circuit!

Mnemonic:

  • Short circuit: R low + X strongly negative
  • Open circuit: R high + X strongly positive
  • Acoustically clearly distinguishable!

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Strongly positive phase (approx. +170° to +180°)
  • Almost at +180° (phase reversal, different direction from short circuit!)
  • Varies slightly over frequency

Meaning:

  • Phase ≈ +180° = Complete reflection with positive phase reversal
  • Opposite of short circuit (-180°)!
  • TDR signature: Positive reflection
  • THIRD AUDIBLE DIFFERENCE!

🎯 Smith Chart Audio Visualization

Activate Smith Visualization

  1. In acoustic mode: Press V key
  2. Press B key to select mode:
  • Recommended: Mode 4 (SWR Circles)
  • Alternative: Mode 1 (Cartesian) - shows left/right difference!

Expected Smith Audio Cues

Mode 4: SWR Circles

What you should hear:

  • Sound VERY FAR AWAY from you (like Scenario 8)
  • Almost at edge of Smith chart
  • No audible difference from short circuit in this mode!

Meaning:

  • SWR circle mode only shows distance (SWR magnitude)
  • Does NOT distinguish between short circuit/open circuit
  • Need different mode for distinction!

Mode 1: Cartesian ⭐ RECOMMENDED FOR DISTINCTION!

What you should hear:

  • Sound FAR RIGHT (Re(Γ) ≈ +1.0) ← OPPOSITE of short circuit!
  • Sound SLIGHTLY UP (Im(Γ) > 0)
  • Position near right edge of Smith chart

Meaning:

  • Re(Γ) ≈ +1 = Almost complete reflection, positive real part
  • Im(Γ) > 0 = Phase > 0 (open circuit is in upper half)
  • Right outside = Open circuit position in Smith chart
  • CLEARLY DISTINGUISHABLE from short circuit (left outside)!

Comparison:

Short Circuit (Scenario 8): Sound FAR LEFT

Open Circuit (Scenario 9):  Sound FAR RIGHT → Maximum horizontal distance in Smith chart!

Mode 3: Impedance Direct

What you should hear:

  • Sound EXTREMELY FAR RIGHT (R → ∞, very high impedance)
  • Sound FAR UP (X strongly positive)
  • Position: Right upper corner of impedance space

Meaning:

  • Right side = High impedance (R → ∞)
  • Up = Inductive (X positive)
  • Right upper corner = Open circuit signature!

Comparison:

Short Circuit: LEFT LOWER Corner (R→0, X negative)

Open Circuit:  RIGHT UPPER Corner (R→∞, X positive) → Diagonally opposite in impedance space!

Center Pulse (Reference Signal)

Activate:

  1. Press A key in acoustic mode → Audio configuration
  2. Menu: C → Smith Audio → Enable Center Pulse

What you hear:

  • Periodic pulse from CENTER (50Ω)
  • Your measurement is VERY FAR AWAY from pulse (like short circuit)
  • But: In different direction! (Right instead of left)

🔧 Exercises and Learning Tasks

Exercise 1: Short Circuit vs. Open Circuit - Direct Comparison! ⭐

Goal: Learn to distinguish acoustically

  1. Load Scenario 8 (short circuit)
  • SWR: Very high (same)
  • Impedance |Z|: Very low tone (low impedance)
  • Reactance X: Strongly negative
  • Smith Cartesian: Far LEFT
  1. Load Scenario 9 (open circuit)
  • SWR: Very high (same)
  • Impedance |Z|: Very high tone (high impedance)
  • Reactance X: Strongly positive
  • Smith Cartesian: Far RIGHT
  1. Switch multiple times and internalize differences!

Mnemonic:

SWR says: "Something is very wrong!"

Impedance says: "It's short circuit!" (low) or "It's open circuit!" (high)

Exercise 2: TDR Localization for Open Circuit

Goal: Calculate fault distance (like Scenario 8, but different distance) Expectation:

  • Cable: 5 meters (half as long as Scenario 8)
  • Phase shift should be smaller

Practical:

  1. U-Menu → Key 4 → Read impedance at 145 MHz
  2. Note phase
  3. Calculation: (see Scenario 8)
  • Result should be ~5m (not 10m like Scenario 8)

Exercise 3: Smith Position as Diagnostic Tool

Goal: Determine fault type from Smith position Exercise:

  1. Activate only Smith visualization (Mode 1)
  2. Turn off SWR (Key 1)
  3. Listen only to Smith position
  4. Question: Where is the sound?
  • Far left: Short circuit!
  • Far right: Open circuit!
  • Central: Good match
  • Moderately far: Mismatch (but not a fault)

Exercise 4: Practical Fault Diagnosis

Goal: Workflow for real fault finding Scenario:

  • Antenna suddenly stops working
  • SWR meter shows SWR >> 10:1

Diagnostic Steps:

  1. VNA S11 sweep: SWR everywhere high
  2. Check impedance |Z|:
  • Very low (<5Ω): → Short circuit → Search for damage
  • Very high (>1000Ω): → Open circuit → Search for break
  1. TDR calculation: Determine fault distance
  2. Physical inspection:
  • Short circuit: Cable sheath damaged? Water? Connector?
  • Open circuit: Antenna element broken? Connector loose? Corrosion?
  1. Repair: According to fault type
  2. Re-test: Should work again!

Exercise 5: Understanding Impedance Quadrants

Goal: Learn four Smith regions Impedance Space:

        Inductive (X > 0)

| Low Impedance -+- High Impedance (R < 50)    |    (R > 50) | Capacitive (X < 0)

Fault Types:

  • Short circuit: Low impedance + Capacitive (left lower corner)
  • Open circuit: High impedance + Inductive (right upper corner)
  • Antenna too short: Slightly high impedance + Inductive (right-up from center)
  • Antenna too long: Slightly low impedance + Capacitive (left-down from center)

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

What You Learn from this Scenario

1. Open Circuit Recognition

Acoustic Signatures:

  • SWR: Extremely high (like short circuit - no difference!)
  • Impedance |Z|: Very high tone (>> 50Ω) ← Difference!
  • Reactance X: Strongly positive ← Difference!
  • Smith Cartesian: Far right ← Difference!
  • Phase: ~+180° ← Difference!

2. Short Circuit vs. Open Circuit Distinction

Only possible with impedance analysis!

              | Short Circuit    | Open Circuit

--------------|------------------|-------------- SWR           | Very high       | Very high (same!) R             | → 0Ω           | → ∞ X             | Strongly negative| Strongly positive Smith (Cart.) | Left outside    | Right outside Phase         | -180°           | +180° Audio Z       | Very low tone   | Very high tone Audio X       | Low (negative)  | High (positive)

3. TDR Application

  • Both fault types localizable with TDR
  • Phase sign distinguishes fault type:
  • Negative (-180°): Short circuit
  • Positive (+180°): Open circuit
  • Phase magnitude: Gives distance

4. Smith Chart Symmetry

  • Short circuit and open circuit: Symmetric around center
  • Left-right axis: Separates low/high impedance
  • Up-down axis: Separates inductive/capacitive
  • Extreme positions: Always indicate fault!

5. Practical Relevance

  • Open circuit more frequent than expected:
  • Oxidized contacts (years old)
  • Antenna elements break (storm, ice)
  • Connector center contact withdrawn
  • Diagnosis: Impedance measurement essential
  • Repair: Often easier than short circuit (restore connection)

⚠️ Important Notes

Open Circuit Causes in Practice

Most Common Causes:

  1. Oxidation (gradual!)
  • Contacts oxidize over years
  • Resistance increases → Eventually break
  • Typical for outdoor installations
  1. Mechanical Break
  • Antenna elements break (aluminum fatigues)
  • Cable inner conductor tears (kink, overstretching)
  • Connector center contact breaks off
  1. Loose Connections
  • Screw connections loosen (vibration)
  • Connector not fully inserted
  • Solder joint gone cold

Prevention:

  • Corrosion protection: Contact spray, solder with flux
  • Mechanical strain relief: No tension on cable
  • Regular inspection: Check antenna elements annually
  • Quality connectors: Cheap connectors are fault source #1

Difference: High Impedance Load vs. Open Circuit

High Impedance Mismatch (e.g., 150Ω):

  • SWR ~3:1 (moderately bad)
  • Still usable (with tuner)
  • Smith: Right of center, but not at edge

Open Circuit (∞):

  • SWR >> 10:1 (catastrophic)
  • Not usable (even with tuner)
  • Smith: At right edge

Acoustically:

  • Mismatch: Impedance tone moderately high
  • Open circuit: Impedance tone extremely high
