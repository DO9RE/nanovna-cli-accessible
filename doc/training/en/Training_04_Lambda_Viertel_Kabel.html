Training Scenario 4: Quarter-Wave Cable as Impedance Transformer

Training file: Training/04_lambda_viertel_kabel.csv Frequency range: 144.000 - 146.000 MHz (2m Amateur Radio Band) Difficulty level: ⭐⭐⭐ Advanced Learning objective: Understanding impedance transformation through quarter-wave lines and rotation in the Smith chart

📋 Scenario Description

Fictitious Component

You are measuring a 25Ω antenna through a λ/4 coaxial cable at 144 MHz. The cable is exactly λ/4 (approx. 52 cm at v=0.66) long and transforms the 25Ω impedance into a higher impedance. At the resonance frequency, a 180° rotation in the Smith chart occurs.

Technical Properties

  • Impedance at cable input:
  • 144.0 MHz: 25.0Ω + j0.0Ω (low impedance, resistive)
  • 144.5 MHz: ~50Ω (through λ/4 transformation)
  • 146.0 MHz: ~100Ω (high impedance, resistive)
  • SWR: 2.00 to 2.90 (varies due to transformation effect)
  • Return Loss: 4.00 to 6.02 dB (moderate)
  • Reactance (X): Oscillates due to line resonance
  • Special feature: Spectacular Smith chart rotation by 180°

Physical Meaning

A λ/4 cable (quarter-wave transformer) is a fundamental RF concept:

  • Impedance transformation: Z_in = Z₀² / Z_L
  • With Z₀=50Ω and Z_L=25Ω: Z_in = 50²/25 = 100Ω
  • Practical application: Matching low-impedance antennas
  • Smith effect: Complete rotation around chart center at λ/4

Typical Applications:

  • Matching ground plane antennas (35Ω → 50Ω)
  • Stacking harness for multi-antenna arrays
  • Impedance matching in filters and amplifiers
  • J-Pole antenna matching section

🎵 Expected Audio Events

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Medium tone at 144 MHz (SWR 2.00)
  • Tone rises slightly to approx. 144.5 MHz (SWR 2.50)
  • Then continues rising to 146 MHz (SWR 2.90)
  • Wave-like movement in stereo field

Meaning:

  • SWR varies due to impedance transformation of λ/4 cable
  • No perfect match at a single frequency
  • Transformation effect visible across entire band
  • Typical "pass-through" behavior through transformation range

Position in stereo field:

  • Sound moves dynamically through stereo field
  • Shows impedance "migration" through λ/4 transformation

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Low to medium tone (4-6 dB range)
  • Slight variation across frequency
  • Correlates with SWR behavior
  • No dramatic peaks

Meaning:

  • Moderate return loss = Moderate reflections
  • λ/4 transformation is frequency-dependent
  • Shows "bandwidth limitation" of transformer

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Very low tone at 144 MHz (25Ω)
  • Tone rises dramatically across frequency
  • Medium tone at 144.5 MHz (~50Ω)
  • High tone at 146 MHz (~100Ω)
  • Strong, continuous upward trend

Meaning:

  • Shows classic λ/4 impedance transformation!
  • From low impedance (25Ω) to high impedance (100Ω)
  • Passes through 50Ω system impedance at band center
  • This is the core effect of this scenario!

Acoustic landmark:

  • One of the most dramatic impedance sweeps in all training scenarios
  • Sounds like "ascending siren" in triangle wave sound

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth)

What you should hear:

  • Oscillating tone around zero line
  • Alternates between slightly inductive and slightly capacitive
  • Transition from positive → zero → negative → zero → positive possible
  • Wave movement characteristic of line resonance

Meaning:

  • Reactance oscillates through line resonances
  • At exactly λ/4: X should theoretically be zero
  • Frequency detuning leads to reactance fluctuations
  • Shows "resonance pass-through" of transformer

Acoustic character:

  • Sounds like "wave-like up and down" in sawtooth
  • Clearly differs from monotonous trends (Scenarios 2+3)

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Dramatic phase change across frequency
  • Can show large jumps (±45° or more)
  • Transition from positive → zero → negative
  • Strong dynamics in phase progression

Meaning:

  • Phase changes strongly through line transformation
  • At λ/4: 180° phase rotation expected
  • Shows electrical length of cable
  • Important for understanding line theory

Expected Smith Audio Cues

Mode 2: Polar/Radius (RECOMMENDED for this scenario!) ⭐

What you should hear:

  • SPECTACULAR ROTATION around the Smith chart center!
  • Sound rotates continuously in 3D space
  • Circular movement from left through front to right
  • 180° rotation across frequency range
  • Radius remains relatively constant (distance from center)
  • Rotation speed is uniform

Meaning:

  • Rotation = λ/4 line effect in Smith chart!
  • Complete orbit = line length = λ/2
  • Half orbit = line length = λ/4
  • This is THE characteristic acoustic signal for line transformation!

Audio experience:

  • Like a rotating sound around your head
  • Begins left (144 MHz)
  • Moves forward (band center)
  • Ends right (146 MHz)
  • Unforgettable Acoustic Signature!

Mode 1: Cartesian

What you should hear:

  • Sound makes circular movement through space
  • Begins at Re(Γ) negative (left)
  • Moves through Im(Γ) positive/negative (front/back)
  • Ends at Re(Γ) positive (right)
  • Elliptical trajectory possible

Meaning:

  • Reflection coefficient wanders around Smith center
  • Shows same rotation as Mode 2, but in Cartesian coordinates
  • More complex audio interpretation

Mode 3: Impedance Direct

What you should hear:

  • Horizontal movement from left to right (R: 25Ω → 100Ω)
  • Slight vertical oscillation (X: inductive ↔ capacitive)
  • Sound glides from left (low impedance) to right (high impedance)
  • Vertical position wobbles around center

Meaning:

  • Resistance R rises continuously (main effect)
  • Reactance X oscillates (secondary effect)
  • Shows transformation directly in R+jX space

Center Pulse (Reference Signal)

Activate:

  1. Key A in acoustic mode → Audio configuration
  2. Menu: C → Smith Audio → Activate Center Pulse

What you hear:

  • Periodic pulse from the CENTER (reference = 50Ω + j0)
  • Your measurement rotates AROUND this pulse!
  • When measurement crosses pulse: Impedance = 50Ω reached
  • Shows distance to perfect match

1. Understanding λ/4 Impedance Transformation

  • Impedance formula: Z_in = Z₀² / Z_L
  • Frequency dependence: Only optimal at exact λ/4
  • Bandwidth limitation: Narrowband compared to resistive antennas
  • Practical meaning: Matching low-impedance loads

2. Experience Smith Chart Rotation Acoustically

  • 180° rotation at λ/4 line = characteristic behavior
  • Continuous rotation = line effect
  • Circle around center = Constant reflection coefficient magnitude
  • This is THE difference between antennas and lines!

3. Acoustic Signatures for Transformation

  • Impedance |Z|: Dramatic upward trend = transformation active
  • Smith Polar mode: Rotating sound = line transformation
  • Reactance X: Oscillation = line resonances
  • Phase: Strong change = electrical length

4. Difference: Antenna vs. Line

  • Antenna (Scenarios 1-3): Static impedance or monotonous trend
  • Line (Scenario 4): Cyclic transformation, rotation
  • Combination (practice): Antenna + line = complex interaction

5. Practical Reference

  • J-Pole antennas: Use λ/4 section for 50Ω matching
  • Stacking harness: 75Ω cable as λ/4 transformer
  • Hybrid combiner: λ/4 lines as isolation elements
  • Impedance measurement: Consideration of cable length important!

⚠️ Important Notes

Reality Check: λ/4 Transformers in Practice

Typical Applications:

  1. Ground plane matching: 35Ω antenna with 42Ω λ/4 cable → 50Ω
  2. Array matching: Four 50Ω antennas parallel = 12.5Ω → 25Ω λ/4 cable → 50Ω
  3. Balun replacement: 1:4 transformation for dipole matching

Bandwidth Problems:

  • λ/4 transformers are narrowband (typically 5-10% bandwidth at SWR<2:1)
  • For wideband applications: Multi-stage transformers (Chebyshev, Binomial)
  • Or: Coaxial stubs with multiple taps

Measurement Technology Aspect:

  • For VNA measurements: Cable length must be known!
  • De-embedding: Calculate out cable effects for true antenna impedance
  • NanoVNA calibration: At antenna feedpoint, not at VNA output!

Technical Details

Impedance Transformation:

Z_in = Z₀² / Z_L

Example: Z₀ = 50Ω (cable impedance) Z_L = 25Ω (load/antenna) Z_in = 50² / 25 = 2500 / 25 = 100Ω

Electrical Length:

λ/4 at 144 MHz:

λ = c / f = 300 / 144 = 2.083 m λ/4 = 0.521 m = 52.1 cm  With velocity factor v=0.66 (RG58): Physical length = 52.1 cm × 0.66 = 34.4 cm

Smith Rotation:

  • λ/4 line: 180° rotation in Smith chart
  • λ/2 line: 360° rotation (full circle) = impedance repetition
  • λ/8 line: 90° rotation

🔬 Deep Dive: Line Theory Acoustically

Why Does It Rotate in the Smith Chart?

Mathematical Background:

Reflection coefficient at VNA:

Γ_in = Γ_L × e^(-j2βl)  β = 2π/λ (phase constant) l = line length  At λ/4: 2βl = 2π/λ × λ/4 × 2 = π e^(-jπ) = -1 (180° phase rotation!)

Acoustic Consequence:

  • Each frequency step = small phase angle step
  • Across band: Continuous phase rotation
  • Smith chart: Rotation manifests as sound movement in space
  • You HEAR the mathematics!

Practical Tip: Determine Cable Length

If you have an unknown cable:

  1. Measure with NanoVNA over wide frequency range
  2. Activate Smith Polar mode
  3. Listen to rotation speed
  4. Fast rotation = short cable
  5. Slow rotation = long cable
  6. One full rotation (360°) = electrical length λ/2

Formula:

l = (Number_of_rotations × λ) / 2
