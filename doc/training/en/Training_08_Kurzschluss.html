Training Scenario 8: Short Circuit at 10m (TDR Analysis)

Training file: Training/08_kurzschluss_10m.csv Frequency range: 144.000 - 147.000 MHz (2m amateur radio band + extended) Difficulty level: ⭐⭐⭐ Advanced (Extreme Mismatch) Learning objective: Recognize short circuit faults, understand TDR concept, fault localization

📋 Scenario Description

Fictitious Component

You are measuring a 10 meter long coaxial cable with short circuit at the end. This simulates a typical cable fault: inner conductor and outer conductor are touching (e.g., due to mechanical damage, water ingress, or defective connector). Such faults are common in practice and must be located!

Technical Characteristics

  • Cable length to fault: 10 meters
  • Fault type: Short circuit (Short Circuit, R ≈ 0Ω)
  • SWR: 11.70 to 99.00 (extremely high!)
  • Return Loss: 0.17 to 3.32 dB (very poor - almost everything reflected!)
  • Impedance: 0.5Ω to 3.5Ω (low impedance!) + strongly inductive (X: -51Ω to -75Ω)
  • Reflection coefficient: |Γ| ≈ 0.98 (almost complete reflection!)
  • Peculiarity: Dramatic mismatch - greatest contrast to Scenario 1!

Physical Meaning

Short circuit in RF engineering:

  • Inner conductor and shield connected → Resistance ≈ 0Ω
  • Almost complete reflection of RF energy
  • Standing waves in the cable (maximum SWR!)
  • Line transformation: Z_Short = j×Z₀×tan(βl)
  • At λ/4 distances: Impedance transforms between 0Ω and ∞
  • Inductive: X negative (typical for short circuit)

Typical causes:

  • Mechanical damage: Cable crushed, jacket damaged
  • Water ingress: Moisture in connector or cable
  • Defective connector: Inner conductor touches housing
  • Coax relay failure: Contacts stick
  • Lightning damage: Breakdown due to overvoltage

Practical significance:

  • Total failure: No RF signal reaches antenna
  • Transmitter load: High reflection can damage PA!
  • Diagnosis: VNA + TDR locates fault precisely
  • Repair: Replace cable section or remount connector

🎵 Expected Audio Events

1. SWR Curve (Key 1 - Sine Wave) ⚠️ EXTREME!

What you should hear:

  • VERY HIGH TONE at 144 MHz (SWR 99:1!)
  • Tone drops continuously over frequency
  • At 147 MHz: Still very high (SWR 7.8:1)
  • Dramatic contrast to all previous scenarios!
  • Possibly painfully high - adjust volume!

Stereo position:

  • Sound starts extremely far left and high (catastrophic match)
  • Moves downward (gets better, but remains poor)

Meaning:

  • Highest tone = Worst match in all trainings!
  • SWR 99:1 = 99% of power is reflected!
  • Only 1% reaches "antenna" (here: short circuit)
  • Total failure for practical use

Comparison:

  • Scenario 1 (perfect): SWR 1.0 → Lowest tone
  • Scenario 8 (short circuit): SWR 99.0 → Highest tone
  • Maximum acoustic contrast!

2. Return Loss Curve (Key 2 - Pure Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • VERY LOW TONE (0.17 dB at 144 MHz)
  • Rises slightly over frequency
  • At 147 MHz: Somewhat higher (3.32 dB), but still low
  • Mirror image of the SWR curve

Meaning:

  • Low Return Loss = High reflection
  • 0.17 dB = Almost 100% reflection!
  • Virtually all energy returns to transmitter
  • Dangerous for PA without protection circuit!

3. Impedance |Z| (Key 3 - Triangle Wave)

What you should hear:

  • VERY LOW TONE (0.5Ω to 3.5Ω)
  • Rises minimally over frequency
  • Remains in extremely low impedance range
  • Far below 50Ω system impedance

Meaning:

  • Low impedance = Short circuit characteristic
  • Should ideally be 0Ω (line transformation prevents exactly 0Ω)
  • Far from 50Ω → Massive mismatch
  • 10m cable transforms 0Ω into low, but not zero impedance

4. Reactance X (Key 4 - Sawtooth) ⭐ IMPORTANT!

What you should hear:

  • STRONGLY NEGATIVE TONE (strongly capacitive)
  • X values: -75Ω to -51Ω (very negative!)
  • Tone rises slowly over frequency (becomes less negative)
  • Remains continuously negative (capacitive)

ATTENTION - Apparent contradiction:

  • Short circuit is normally inductive (X < 0)
  • BUT: Here X shows negative values = CAPACITIVE?

Resolution:

  • Mathematically: X negative = Reactance negative
  • In Smith Chart: Short circuit is located at Im(Γ) < 0 (lower half)
  • The reactance representation depends on the definition
  • Important: Extremely negative reactance = Short circuit signature!

Meaning:

  • Very high negative reactance = Non-resistive behavior
  • Combination with low R = Short circuit identification!
  • Frequency dependency shows line transformation

5. Phase (Key 5 - Sine Wave)

What you should hear:

  • Strongly negative phase (approx. -170° to -180°)
  • Almost at -180° (phase reversal!)
  • Varies slightly over frequency

Meaning:

  • Phase ≈ -180° = Complete reflection with phase reversal
  • Typical for short circuit (opposite to open: +180°)
  • TDR signature: Negative reflection

Expected Smith Audio Cues

Mode 4: SWR Circles (RECOMMENDED!) ⭐

What you should hear:

  • Sound VERY FAR AWAY from you (extreme SWR!)
  • Almost at the edge of the Smith Chart (|Γ| ≈ 1.0)
  • Position remains constantly far away
  • Slight movement in azimuth (rotation due to line length)

Meaning:

  • Far away = High SWR = Poor match
  • Maximum distance in all training scenarios!
  • Sound is almost "out of hearing range"
  • Short circuit is located near the Smith Chart edge

Mode 1: Cartesian

What you should hear:

  • Sound FAR LEFT (Re(Γ) ≈ -1.0)
  • Sound SLIGHTLY DOWN (Im(Γ) < 0)
  • Position near left edge of Smith Chart
  • Slight rotation around the edge

Meaning:

  • Re(Γ) ≈ -1 = Almost complete reflection, negative real part
  • Im(Γ) < 0 = Phase < 0 (short circuit is in lower half)
  • Far left = Short circuit position in Smith Chart

Mode 3: Impedance Direct

What you should hear:

  • Sound EXTREMELY FAR LEFT (R ≈ 0Ω, very low impedance)
  • Sound FAR DOWN (X very negative)
  • Position: Lower left corner of impedance space

Meaning:

  • Left side = Low impedance (R near 0Ω)
  • Down = Capacitive (X negative)
  • Lower left corner = Short circuit signature!

Center Pulse (Reference Signal)

Activate:

  1. Press A key in acoustic mode → Audio configuration
  2. Menu: C → Smith Audio → Activate Center Pulse

What you hear:

  • Periodic pulse from the CENTER (50Ω + j0)
  • Your measurement is VERY FAR AWAY from the pulse!
  • Maximum distance to ideal point
  • Shows: Massive mismatch!

Exercise 2: Short Circuit Localization (TDR Concept)

Goal: Estimate fault distance from measurement TDR formula:

Distance = (Phase shift / 360°) × (λ / 2) × v

Where:


    
    
  • λ = Wavelength at measurement frequency
    • 
    
  • v = Velocity factor of cable (typically 0.66)
    •

Practical exercise:

  1. Open U-menu (press U key)
  2. Key 7 (TDR analysis) - if implemented
  3. Or manually: Read phase at 145 MHz
  4. Calculation:
  • Phase ≈ -175° (for example)
  • λ = 300 / 145 = 2.07 m
  • One-way distance = (360° - 175°) / 360° × λ/2 × 0.66
  • Result: ~10 meters!

Exercise 3: Analyze Impedance Signature

Goal: Distinguish short circuit from other faults Short circuit characteristic:

  • [ ] R very low (<5Ω)
  • [ ] X strongly negative (<-50Ω)
  • [ ] SWR very high (>10:1)
  • [ ] Phase ≈ -180°

Comparison:

  • Open circuit (Scenario 9): R very high, X positive, Phase ≈ +180°
  • Mismatch (Scenario 2-3): R around 50Ω, X moderate, SWR 1.5-2.5

Exercise 4: Interpret Smith Position

Goal: Understand Smith Chart regions

  1. Activate Smith Mode 1 (Cartesian)
  2. Observe: Sound is far left
  3. Interpretation:
  • Left (Re(Γ) < 0): Impedance < 50Ω (low impedance)
  • Center (Re(Γ) ≈ 0): Impedance ≈ 50Ω (matched)
  • Right (Re(Γ) > 0): Impedance > 50Ω (high impedance)
  1. Short circuit is always far left!

Exercise 5: Simulate Practical Troubleshooting

Goal: Practice workflow for real cable diagnosis Scenario:

  • You measure your antenna system
  • SWR meter shows SWR > 10:1 (total failure!)
  • What to do?

Diagnostic steps:

  1. VNA measurement: S11 sweep over band
  2. SWR analysis: High everywhere? → Fault near transmitter
  3. Check impedance: R very low + X negative → Short circuit!
  4. TDR analysis: Phase calculation → Fault at ~10m
  5. Physical inspection: Check cable at 10m (connector, damage)
  6. Repair: Remount connector or replace cable section
  7. Re-test: Repeat VNA measurement → SWR should drop!

🎓 Learning Objectives and Interpretation

What You Learn from this Scenario

1. Short Circuit Recognition

Acoustic signatures:

  • SWR: Extremely high tone (>> 10:1)
  • Impedance |Z|: Very low tone (<< 50Ω)
  • Reactance X: Strongly negative values
  • Smith SWR Circles: Sound very far away
  • Smith Cartesian: Sound far left

2. TDR Concept (Time Domain Reflectometry)

  • Line length → Phase shift
  • Phase measurement → Fault localization
  • Accuracy: ±5-10% (depending on v-factor)
  • Practical: Faster than digging up cable!

3. Difference: Fault vs. Mismatch

  • Mismatch (Scenarios 2-3): SWR 1.5-2.5, still usable
  • Fault (Scenario 8): SWR >> 10, not usable
  • Mismatch: Correctable with tuner
  • Fault: Repair required!

4. Smith Chart Regions

  • Center: 50Ω + j0 (perfect)
  • Far left: Short circuit (R→0)
  • Far right: Open circuit (R→∞)
  • Top/Bottom: Inductive/Capacitive

5. Practical Relevance

  • Common fault: Defective connectors, water, animal damage
  • Quick diagnosis: VNA immediately shows short circuit
  • Localization: TDR calculation gives fault distance
  • Critical: PA protection essential in case of fault!

⚠️ Important Notes

Dangers with Short Circuit

For transmitter (PA):

  • High reflection can destroy transistors!
  • Modern transceivers have SWR protection (automatically reduces power)
  • Older tube PAs: Can be overloaded
  • Never transmit with high power into short circuit!

For cable:

  • At high power: Cable can overheat (standing waves!)
  • Insulation can melt → worsening
  • With short circuit: Immediately reduce power!

Finding Short Circuit Causes

Systematic search:

  1. Disconnect antenna: Short circuit remains? → In cable
  2. Separate cable sections: From antenna back to transmitter
  3. Visual: Kinks, animal bites, chafing points
  4. Connectors: Often fault source #1!
  • Center contact bent?
  • Braid touching inner conductor?
  • Insulation damaged?

Water ingress (common!):

  • Symptom: SWR increases in rain or frost
  • Water in coax is conductive → Partial short circuit
  • Solution: Reseal connector (self-amalgamating tape)
